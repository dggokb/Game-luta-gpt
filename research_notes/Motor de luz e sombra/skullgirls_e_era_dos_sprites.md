# Skullgirls sprite lighting and how sprite-era 2D fighters tint and shadow their sprites

How to read these notes: each claim has a URL and a status.
- **[read]**: the page was fetched and read (through the fetch tool, which allows only short quotes).
- **[snippet]**: only the search snippet was seen.
- **[blocked]**: 403 or unreachable.

skullgirls.com dev-blog permalinks, tcrf.net and neogaf.com thread pages returned 403 to both WebFetch and curl. web.archive.org is not reachable from this network. The two Lab Zero dev-blog posts are therefore known only from search snippets, which do contain verbatim sentences.

## Skullgirls: what each frame stores, how light is computed and combined, stage lights, shadows, mobile

### Takeaway
Primary Lab Zero material confirms the following:
- **Layers:** each Skullgirls frame is three layers: line art, a greyscale shading layer of about 5 grey values, and a flat colour/region map.
- **Combine:** a custom shader combines the layers, deliberately not as a plain multiply. The palette picks a colour range per region, and the shading values select or average within that range.
- **Stage lighting:** the backgrounds have their lighting baked in, while characters are lit at runtime. Each stage file holds an RGB ambient value plus point lights.
- **Brightness rule:** Lab Zero's rule of thumb, reported by a modding guide, is that a character should be about 20% lighter than the stage.
- **Not found:** no primary source was found for the exact rim-light/normal math, for how ground shadows are drawn, or for whether Skullgirls Mobile keeps real-time lights.

### Cited Findings
**Frame data and layer combine (Lab Zero dev blog, 2011)**
- The dev-blog post "Diamonds in the Rough: Animation Clean-Up" (June 2011) describes the shading layer as the second of three clean-up steps. Verbatim: "The shading layer uses about 5 different shades of grey over the lineart."; "A popular line of thought is that the shade layer is a simple multiply layer on top of the color layer. However, any high school art teacher will tell you that shading with grey leaves an image looking muddy and dull."; "we use a custom shader to combine these two layers programmatically to create a final image with a more illustrated look." — [skullgirls.com/2011/06/diamonds-in-the-rough-animation-clean-up/](https://skullgirls.com/2011/06/diamonds-in-the-rough-animation-clean-up/) **[snippet; page blocked 403]**
- The dev-blog post "A Rather Refined Palette" is by Brian Jun, September 2011. It says a character gets a flat colour map marking which area each palette colour affects. Verbatim: "the depth map allows me to choose a range of colors that will be shown on a portion of the sprite and the colors I pick are represented through some averaging, creating that illustrated look that Skullgirls is known for." So a palette entry is a colour *range* per region, indexed by the shading/"depth" value, not a single colour. — [skullgirls.com/2011/09/a-rather-refined-palette/](https://skullgirls.com/2011/09/a-rather-refined-palette/) **[snippet; blocked 403]**
- A fan-made Skullgirls Mobile palette editor gives indirect evidence from the mobile data:
  - Each colour row blends a "detail layer" over a "color layer" with named blend modes. Verbatim: "`Matte` is essentially linear burn."; "`Pastel` … uses an arctangent."; "`Shiny` is basically color dodge."; "`Vivid` is linear burn for low values and linear dodge for high values."; "The game uses more complex and more refined blend modes than the ones available here."
  - Each region is either "opaque (detail layer applies shadows) or transparent (detail layer is an alpha channel)".
  - "Color IDs" (region indices) exist and "were changed in 5.2.0".

  This is reverse-engineered by a third party, not by Hidden Variable, and it covers mobile static art (portraits and cards), not necessarily the in-match sprites. — [github.com/Krazete/sgmpalette](https://github.com/Krazete/sgmpalette) **[read]**

**Per-stage light data (SkullMod guide by modders "Florence" and Cellsai)**
- Verbatim from the guide:
  - "While stage lighting is baked into the textures, character lighting is dynamic."
  - "There's no texture you can change to dim the lights on your character… instead you'll have to edit a stage's character light values."
  - "#ambient light, and the line below it will contain three sets of numbers, three digits each"
  - "These numbers control Maplecrest's ambient lighting; how bright the natural lighting of Maplecrest is, and how said lighting is tinted."
  - The three numbers are R, G and B; "The lower the number, the less of that type of ambient light".
  - "Most stages have additional point light sources".
  - "make sure there is slightly more blue lighting than red or green so the characters are tinted alongside the stage."
- The 20% rule, which the guide attributes to Lab Zero ("this rule of thumb used by Lab Zero themselves"): "A character should be around 20% lighter than the stage they're on so they look like they belong on the stage, but without blending in too much."
- The guide gives no count, colour or position format for the point lights, and says nothing on shadows.
— [steamcommunity.com guide 329234833](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=329234833) **[read]**

### Inferences
- **What one frame carries.** Lab Zero confirms three layers. "depth map" in Jun's post appears to be the same greyscale shading layer, used as an index into a per-region colour ramp:
  - a line layer;
  - a greyscale shading/"depth" layer of about 5 values;
  - a flat region-ID ("colour map") layer.

  This reading is not explicit in the snippets. If the dynamic rim/per-pixel light needs a surface direction, it would most likely come from that greyscale layer treated as height, or from a separate map. No primary source says which.
- **Light model.** Combining the modding guide and the preview gives:
  - Per stage: one RGB ambient term that tints and dims the whole character.
  - A few point lights.
  - Transient lights spawned by hit sparks.
  - The character's own drawn shading is resolved first through the palette ramp, so the light acts on top of finished colours. That ordering is my inference.
- **Design intent.** The 20% rule is the only design-intent statement found: keep characters brighter than the background, tinted to belong without blending in. That is a readability rule.

### Gaps
- No primary Mike Z / Lab Zero statement was found on any of these points:
  - the exact rim-light or normal formula;
  - whether light is added, multiplied or limited to the rim;
  - the number of lights per stage;
  - how hit-spark light falls off.

  The SRK/Skullheart Mike Z threads, his streams and the Indiegogo updates were not found by search. NeoGAF and skullgirls.com posts were blocked.
- The ground-shadow method in Skullgirls was not found in any source.
- Skullgirls Mobile (Hidden Variable): nothing found on whether runtime lighting survives on phones.
- Indivisible (Lab Zero): no source found on its sprite lighting.

## Arc System Works 2D (BlazBlue, P4A, GG XX): sprite production, stage tint, shadows, recolour effects

### Takeaway
ArcSys says BlazBlue uses 3D only as a line/pose guide. Light and shadow are drawn by hand per frame, then pixelled, so the shading is fixed in the art. No source was found on per-stage tinting, how ground shadows are drawn, or palette effects such as burn or freeze.

### Cited Findings
- Junya Motomura, interviewed by Christian Nutt on Gamasutra, 13 Jan 2009, contrasts BlazBlue with KOF:
  - "compared to KOF, the BlazBlue sprites are closer to being hand-drawn"
  - "we only use the 3D graphics as an outline."
  - "In KOF, they do all the shadows and shading in 3D, and then convert them into 2D sprites, but in Blaz Blue, we redraw the sprites by hand."

  — [gamedeveloper.com interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/dodging-striking-winning-the-arc-system-works-interview) **[read]**
- Daisuke Ishiwatari in the same interview: "after we have the animators design things like the shadows, we go in and make corrections ourselves." — [same](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/dodging-striking-winning-the-arc-system-works-interview) **[read]**
- Toshimichi Mori in the same interview: "the graphics in a 2D fighter have to retain that hand-drawn feel, no matter what technology is being used… even if we're starting out with 3D models, we have to go back over them by hand". — [same](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/dodging-striking-winning-the-arc-system-works-interview) **[read]**
- Siliconera, "The Art of BlazBlue Part 2" (9 Feb 2012), gives the pipeline:
  1. "each animation frame's pose is drawn by hand";
  2. "A 3D model is made of each character and posed";
  3. it is "used to create a consistent 2D line image as a guide";
  4. "Light and shadow is then applied and additional detail worked into each individual frame…";
  5. "…which is then turned into the dot image – the sprite itself."

  — [siliconera.com](https://www.siliconera.com/the-art-of-blazblue-part-2-animation-phase/) **[read]**
- The Guilty Gear wiki says some Eddie palettes "do not use fully black shadows". This shows shadow colour is a palette entry, but it refers to Eddie's shadow-body, not a ground shadow. — [guiltygear.wiki.gg](https://guiltygear.wiki.gg/wiki/Eddie/Gallery) **[snippet]**

### Inferences
- BlazBlue's sprite shading is drawn into the pixels. Any stage response would have to be a programmatic colour operation on the whole sprite (as KOF XIII documents below), not relighting.

### Gaps
- No source was found on any of these for BlazBlue, Persona 4 Arena or Guilty Gear XX:
  - per-stage character tint or darkening;
  - how the ground shadow is drawn (flattened silhouette or blob, colour, alpha);
  - hit flash, burn, freeze or poison recolours;
  - the dark silhouette during super freeze.

  Dustloop and Mizuumi technical pages did not come up in searches. Persona 4 Arena was not specifically covered.

## SNK KOF XII/XIII: dot pipeline, stage-dependent colour, shadows

### Takeaway
KOF XII/XIII render posed 3D models with diffuse-only toon lighting. Artists then hand-pixel the expressions, shading and specular highlights. The finished sprites get **no runtime lighting**: scene darkening and flashes are "programmatic colour manipulation". SNK's own Global Match page says sprites show "subtle color variation to reflect background tones" and "visible glare from in-game light sources".

### Cited Findings
**4Gamer, KOF XIII developer interview (Zenji Nishikawa, 25 Aug 2010; Yamamoto and Kukino)**
- 3D first: 「まず3Dモデルを用いたポージングやライティングなどを行って描画を行い，それを2Dフレームとして出力。」 (first posing and lighting with 3D models, then output as 2D frames). After that, a post-process hand-draws 「イラストテイストの表情や陰影」 (illustration-style expressions and shading) as dot art.
- The interviewer describes this as hand-made NPR shading.
- 「3Dモデルへのライティングは拡散反射系のトゥーンシェーディングのみが行われていて，鏡面反射のハイライトは手付けで行われている」 (the 3D lighting is diffuse toon shading only; specular highlights are placed by hand). The interviewer calls this a KOF tradition since '94.
- Costume textures on the 3D models exist only to show colour divisions.
- No runtime lighting, and stage/effect colour is programmatic: 「完成形の2Dのドットグラフィックスは，ランタイムにおいて，3Dグラフィックスパイプラインのリアルタイムライティングが行われることはない」 (the finished dot graphics never get real-time lighting at runtime) and 「シーンの明暗変化や閃光などのエフェクト表現で色味が変わることがあるが，それらは，あくまでプログラム的な色操作によるもの」 (colour changes from scene light/dark shifts or flashes are purely programmatic colour operations).
- The article does not mention palettes or colour counts.

— [4gamer.net](https://www.4gamer.net/games/111/G011100/20100820077/index_2.html) **[read, page 2]**

**SNK official KOF XIII Global Match "2D dot" page (EN)**
- "The pixel art in this title uses light and shadow effects extensively to give more heft to on-screen elements."
- It lists "subtle dynamic touches" on sprites: "visible glare from in-game light sources" and "subtle color variation to reflect background tones".

— [snk-corp.co.jp/us/games/kof13gm/2ddot/](https://snk-corp.co.jp/us/games/kof13gm/2ddot/) **[read]**

**KOF XII press**
- KOF XII was reported as "100% hand-drawn, no cel shading", at roughly 1.5 frames per designer per day. — [gamespark.jp](https://www.gamespark.jp/modules/news/index.php?p=5224) **[snippet]**; [techgage.com](https://techgage.com/news/king_of_fighters_xii_is_100_hand-drawn/) **[snippet]**

### Inferences
- The SNK page and 4Gamer agree if KOF XIII's "background-tone colour variation" and "glare" are global colour operations, such as tint, brightness or additive flash, applied per stage or per event. The page does not say whether this is palette-based or a shader multiply.
- Either way, it is not per-pixel lighting. This is the cheapest documented model: fixed drawn shading plus a per-stage tint.

### Gaps
- How the per-stage tint is parameterised (palette swap or colour multiply, values per stage): not found.
- The KOF XII/XIII shadow method: not found.

## Stated rationale: fixed character colours vs tinting

### Takeaway
Two developer-side positions are documented:
- **Lab Zero tints characters per stage, but bounded:** about 20% brighter than the stage, "so they look like they belong… without blending in too much".
- **SNK keeps the drawn shading fixed:** no runtime lighting, only programmatic colour shifts and "subtle" variation to match background tones.

ArcSys stresses keeping the "hand-drawn feel", but says nothing on tinting.

### Cited Findings
- The Lab Zero 20% rule, as reported by modders. — [steamcommunity.com guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=329234833) **[read]**
- KOF XIII has no runtime lighting, and its colour changes are programmatic. — [4gamer.net](https://www.4gamer.net/games/111/G011100/20100820077/index_2.html) **[read]**
- SNK's sprites get "subtle color variation to reflect background tones". — [snk-corp.co.jp](https://snk-corp.co.jp/us/games/kof13gm/2ddot/) **[read]**
- Mori on the "hand-drawn feel, no matter what technology is being used". — [gamedeveloper.com](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/dodging-striking-winning-the-arc-system-works-interview) **[read]**
- Players on Skullgirls' Class Notes stage wanted lighting removed so characters "look exactly like their corner portraits". This is a player-side readability complaint about strong stage tint. — [skullheart.com](https://skullheart.com/threads/skullgirls-stage-background-discussion.1127/) **[read]**

### Inferences
- The two documented developer positions both keep the tint small:
  - Lab Zero: dynamic, but with characters about 20% brighter than the stage.
  - SNK: "subtle", programmatic only.
- No source endorses strong stage relighting of painted sprites.

### Gaps
- No explicit ArcSys or French-Bread statement on why character colours stay fixed was found.
