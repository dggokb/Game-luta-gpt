# Arc System Works: how the VFX of its anime-style fighting games are made (primary sources)

Reading notes for the report writer:

- "PRIMARY" = the developers' own words (talk recording, official ASW video, interview quote). "SECONDARY" = a journalist's write-up of ASW material. "THIRD-PARTY" = outside analysis.
- Four of the most useful sources are official Arc System Works YouTube videos ("ASW Academy"). I read them through YouTube's auto-generated Japanese captions, which contain many homophone errors (e.g. 軌跡 "trajectory" is captioned as 奇跡, ディザリング as リザリング, 手描き as 手かけ). Translations below are mine, with the obvious caption errors corrected. Where the caption is too garbled to be sure, I say so. Timestamps are approximate (start of a ~30 s caption block).
- The GDC 2015 quotes are from the English auto-captions of the official GDC recording; wording is verbatim apart from removed "um"s.
- I could not open: the GDC handout PDF (ggxrd.com, connection reset), the Unreal Engine developer interview on Strive (HTTP 403), the CGWORLD 2021 session page (HTTP 503), and the two dragonball.news DBFZ developer articles (unreachable, also via web archive). ## 1. Guilty Gear Xrd (GDC 2015 talk, 4Gamer 2014, CEDEC 2015): how are effects made?

### Takeaway
Xrd effects are a mix of three things: 2D flipbook "animated particles" (hit effects are a set of pictures switched every frame), 3D effect meshes modelled by hand one frame at a time and swapped per frame (smoke, explosion smoke, Zato's shadow melt), and one-frame extra meshes for weapon smears. Pure effects are unlit, have shading baked into the texture and no outline, and the characters' limited-animation rule (no interpolation, every frame a hand-posed key, per-pose hold times of 1 to 4 frames at 60 fps) is the model the effects follow.

### Cited Findings
- PRIMARY (Motomura, GDC 2015 Q&A, ~47:30). Asked "if the effects are hand animated the same way or if they are particles", he first says "I couldn't really go into effects today", then: "most of the effects are particles, but not just particles flying around, but animated particles. So the hit effects and everything like that are actually 2D but have a set of pictures switching every frame to get the animation." — [GDC 2015 talk recording, official GDC channel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yhGjCzxJV3E) (also on [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022031/GuiltyGearXrd-s-Art-Style-The))
- PRIMARY (same answer, ~48:00). On smoke: "such as the smoke from the character's feet when they jump ... they are actually done in a pretty kind of a brute force way. They modeled each frame and swapped each frame. So we have a bunch of meshes swapping every frame as a smoke." He adds "it actually isn't that hard to do", and that it began as a joke ("we're going to model them each frame") "and it turned out to be the best way actually." — [GDC 2015 talk recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yhGjCzxJV3E)
- PRIMARY (same Q&A, ~49:55). On sword smears ("drag on the shape" when swinging swords): it is "a combination with switching meshes"; "some characters' swords are switched when they are kind of expanded and other times we just add an extra mesh of that shape because it only lasts for like a frame on the screen. So it's no point putting the mesh into the actual mesh ... we just add it as an effect." — [GDC 2015 talk recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yhGjCzxJV3E)
- PRIMARY (same Q&A, ~44:30). Model swapping also covers character-attached shapes: "shadows of Zato, hairs of Millia ... they do a lot of morphing animation and that is practically impossible to just do by a [single mesh]". — [GDC 2015 talk recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yhGjCzxJV3E)
- SECONDARY with PRIMARY quotes (4Gamer, Zenji Nishikawa, July 2014, part 2, section "3Dモデリングされたエフェクト群"). "Most effects attached to special moves, with some exceptions, are 3D-modelled": specifically "smoke, flame, various flashes, guard effects" (煙，火炎，各種閃光，ガードエフェクト). — [4Gamer, Xrd graphics part 2](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140714079/)
- PRIMARY quote in that article (Daisuke Ishiwatari), the reason: in finishers the camera moves in 3D, and "if the effects were paper-thin billboards there it felt uncool, so we boldly made the effects 3D models too" (エフェクトがビルボードでペラペラだと格好悪いと感じたので，思い切ってエフェクト群も3Dモデルにしてみました). — [4Gamer, Xrd graphics part 2](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140714079/)
- SECONDARY (same article). The smoke "looks like a small fluid animation" but "is modelled frame by frame by an artist's hands" (アーティストの手によって1コマ1コマモデリングされている); the explosion smoke that rises, swells and bursts is also made frame by frame. Because it is 3D data, its look changes when the camera moves. The article shows a wireframe of the jump smoke. — [4Gamer, Xrd graphics part 2](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140714079/)
- PRIMARY quote (Motomura, same article). "At first we experimented with moving several sphere models connected by bones, but it never became something we were satisfied with ... in the end it became brute force" (力ワザ). For Zato's shadow melt, the character model is deformed part-way, then swapped for a separately prepared "shadow melt" effect model. — [4Gamer, Xrd graphics part 2](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140714079/)
- SECONDARY (same article), lighting rules for effects. Pure effects such as smoke are not lit; their shading is baked into the texture; the outline process is not applied. Emissive effects (flame, flashes) only look bright and do not light their surroundings, because "in a 2D fighter, if an effect's look changed with conditions, it could interfere with play feel". Effects that are parts of a character (Zato's shadow melt) are lit. ASW said it would keep judging 3D effects cautiously. — [4Gamer, Xrd graphics part 2](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140714079/)
- PRIMARY quote (Motomura, same article). Effects are also used to hide problems: in some throws "we cover it up with a flame effect" (炎のエフェクトで覆い隠してごまかしている箇所も). — [4Gamer, Xrd graphics part 2](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140714079/)
- PRIMARY (Motomura, GDC 2015, ~30:00), the limited-animation rule: "Having the poses interpolate between key frames makes it look smoother, but at the same time makes it look more 3D. So we just stopped using interpolations between key frames. Every frame now is a key frame ... You could imagine stop motion animation." "No simulation was used because again, it just doesn't look 2D." — [GDC 2015 talk recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yhGjCzxJV3E)
- PRIMARY (Motomura, GDC 2015, ~31:20). "Turning off interpolations between key frames is essential. But that alone is not enough ... The secret is to deform the mesh every key frame to add imperfection." Scale animation "was used a lot"; the engine did not support it, so ASW implemented it. — [GDC 2015 talk recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yhGjCzxJV3E)
- PRIMARY quote (Hidehiko Sakamura, 4Gamer). Cutscenes use 15 pictures per second as the base ("slightly more than cel anime's 8 to 12"). In battle, how many frames each pose is shown is set per pose in script and is not constant, e.g. "2F, 3F, 3F, 1F, 1F, 2F, 2F, 3F, 4F" (1F ≈ 16.67 ms). — [4Gamer, Xrd graphics part 2](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140714079/)
- PRIMARY quote (Sakamura, 4Gamer). Thinning out a 60 fps F-curve animation was tried and rejected: it "only looked like 3D graphics dropping frames" (コマ落ちした3Dグラフィックス). — [4Gamer, Xrd graphics part 2](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140714079/)
- SECONDARY (4Gamer). A storyboard for a special move states frame specs (e.g. total 60 frames, hit active from frame 30); May's example board reads "20 drawings for 54 frames". Exceptions that update at a true 60 fps: the parabolic movement of jumps and the movement of projectiles. — [4Gamer, Xrd graphics part 2](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140714079/)
- PRIMARY (Motomura, GDC 2015, ~43:15). The camera is not stepped: "camera rotation ... looks ugly with less frames"; in anime the characters run at "eight or 12" per second but the background moves at 24; "we tried lower frame camera movement, but it just didn't feel good." — [GDC 2015 talk recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yhGjCzxJV3E)
- SECONDARY (4Gamer), post effects. FXAA, light bloom (64-bit float HDR target) and a separate diffusion filter applied after bloom. Ishiwatari: strong post effects are mainly for cutscenes, because a hazy look in the fighting part "makes it hard to play". — [4Gamer, Xrd graphics part 2](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140714079/)
- SECONDARY (Famitsu report of Sakamura's CEDEC 2015 talk "Real-time limited animation in GUILTY GEAR Xrd"). Cutscenes are built in Autodesk Softimage; the engine played animation by seconds, so programmers changed it to frame-based playback to keep character and camera in step; each frame gets small hand adjustments to imitate hand-drawn wobble. The report does not cover effects. — [Famitsu, CEDEC 2015](https://www.famitsu.com/news/201508/27087024.html)

### Inferences
- The two sources describe the mix differently. Motomura (2015) says "most" effects are flipbook particles and names smoke as the modelled exception; the 4Gamer article (2014) says "most" move effects are 3D-modelled and lists smoke, flame, flashes and guard effects. They are compatible if hit sparks are flipbooks on particles and the larger volumetric shapes are meshes, but neither source gives a count, so the true proportion is unknown.
- No Xrd source says in so many words "effects are animated on twos/threes". What is stated is that hit effects switch picture "every frame" and smoke meshes swap "every frame"; whether "frame" means every 1/60 s or every animation step is not specified.
- Stepped, non-interpolated playback is applied to effects in practice (mesh swap per frame is by definition non-interpolated), but the explicit "no interpolation" statements are about characters.

### Gaps
- The GDC handout PDF could not be opened, so I cannot say whether its slides add effect details beyond the Q&A.
- No number found for Xrd effect texture sizes, flipbook frame counts, number of smoke meshes, or hitstop frames.
- Nothing found on how Xrd slash trails are textured (UV scroll, alpha erosion); the only statement is the one-frame extra mesh above.
- The effect authoring tool for Xrd (Unreal Engine 3's Cascade or a custom tool) is not named in any source I read.

## 2. Later games: Dragon Ball FighterZ, Granblue Fantasy Versus, Guilty Gear Strive (engine, tools, technique)

### Takeaway
Guilty Gear Strive is by far the best documented: lead effect designer Kazuya Ogawa's 2021 CGWORLD talk (republished by ASW in 2025) and his 2026 "how fighting-game effects are made" lecture describe a mesh-plus-hand-drawn-texture pipeline in Unreal Engine 4, with dithering, vertex animation textures, parallax occlusion mapping and hand-drawn frame-by-frame overlays. For Dragon Ball FighterZ and Granblue Fantasy Versus I found no developer statement about effect construction at all, only engine and animation-timing facts.

### Cited Findings

Guilty Gear Strive, battle effects (PRIMARY: Kazuya Ogawa, "Effect production techniques in GUILTY GEAR -STRIVE- Part 1", ASW official video published 8 Oct 2025, recording of the CGWORLD 2021 Creative Conference talk)
- Hand-drawn work remains central: after showing the material-based techniques he says "to produce staging with impact, the hand-drawn method is indispensable" and that in cutscenes they "draw on top of the screen like cel animation", something "indispensable since past Guilty Gear titles". — [ASW Academy, Strive effects Part 1, ~10:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vha6dkrVvZ0)
- Hand-drawn overlay workflow: the motion team hands over the finished shot (sometimes with a rough mesh marking where the effect goes); a 2D-team artist draws the animation frame by frame over it in drawing software; blur or glow is added in video-editing software; the 2D team delivers a numbered image sequence; it is imported into the engine, given a dedicated material that fits it to the screen (with offset and scale controls) and played in the effect editor; filler effects and post-process are added last. — [ASW Academy, Strive effects Part 1, ~11:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vha6dkrVvZ0)
- Frame rate of those overlays: "Cutscenes are basically made at 1 drawing per 4 frames, but from this title the background itself moves at full frame." (At 60 fps that is 15 drawings per second.) — [ASW Academy, Strive effects Part 1, ~11:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vha6dkrVvZ0)
- Texture cost of hand-drawn sequences: when resolution will not fit in one sheet it is split across several; colour is sometimes changed per character via a data table; stated downsides are "increased texture size" and difficulty following later changes, but it is used "at the decisive moments" for its expressive power. (Caption partly garbled.) — [ASW Academy, Strive effects Part 1, ~11:50 to 13:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vha6dkrVvZ0)
- Dithering instead of translucency: a dither shader is applied to effect meshes "where translucent materials are hard to use", e.g. a mesh that wraps around a character and needs different colours on front and back faces, which translucency cannot sort correctly. It is also used to fade out debris and 3D smoke, "which until now we shrank or sank into the ground"; back polygons do not show through, and because it is a masked material the draw cost is low. — [ASW Academy, Strive effects Part 1, ~04:00 to 05:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vha6dkrVvZ0)
- Vertex animation textures (VAT): mesh animation (vertex position and rotation) is baked into textures; used for the base mesh of magic circles and simple destruction simulations. Workflow: animate the mesh in a DCC tool, export image plus mesh with a dedicated script, import, apply the material, check in the effect editor. Data grows with vertex count and frame count. One example combines several VAT meshes (expanding ring, lines, radial spike burst, lightning-shaped mesh) into the clash effect shown when both players' attacks hit at the same time. Stated benefit: meshes keep resolution when the camera comes close and hold up from any angle. — [ASW Academy, Strive effects Part 1, ~07:55 to 10:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vha6dkrVvZ0)
- Parallax occlusion mapping: ground cracks for a ground-slam move are a single flat polygon with one height map plus emissive; limitation is very shallow camera angles. Also applied to polygons covering a character to show cracking. — [ASW Academy, Strive effects Part 1, ~05:40 to 07:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vha6dkrVvZ0)
- SECONDARY confirmation of the above four techniques and of the talk's origin (CGWORLD 2021 Creative Conference, "GUILTY GEAR -STRIVE- background & effect artwork"). — [GameMakers, 10 Oct 2025](https://gamemakers.jp/article/2025_10_10_120081/)

Guilty Gear Strive, story-mode effects (PRIMARY: same talk, Part 2)
- Scale: story mode is about 4.5 hours of real-time animation; the effect list was managed in a spreadsheet by category; "including variants, the final count was about 1,000 effects". Hit effects and environment effects were started early because they do not depend on motion. — [ASW Academy, Strive effects Part 2, ~00:40 to 02:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nTZM-1SDAp4)
- About 20 post-process materials were built (he compares post-process to anime "photography/compositing"), each limited to 4 parameters so the staging team could use them: halation at a chosen position and radius, burn-in contrast, speed lines, concentration lines (with a character-mask variant), film noise, glitch noise, outline extraction, white-out. They are "not accurate calculations" but built for the wanted look. — [ASW Academy, Strive effects Part 2, ~02:20 to 05:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nTZM-1SDAp4)
- A per-scene cap of 50 is stated (the caption reads "object source"; exact noun unclear, it refers to placed effect/object assets), requiring unused assets to be deleted. Effects are placed in a story editor with a game controller. — [ASW Academy, Strive effects Part 2, ~05:20 to 06:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nTZM-1SDAp4)

Guilty Gear Strive, the 2026 lecture (PRIMARY: Kazuya Ogawa, "This is how fighting-game effects are made", two parts, ASW official channel; reported 7 May 2026)
- Speaker: joined ASW in 2003, worked on backgrounds, modelling, then effect designer on the BlazBlue series, now lead effect designer of Guilty Gear Strive. — [ASW Academy lecture, part 1, ~00:10](https://youtu.be/iJwbiIa7D3E); role confirmed by [GameMakers, 7 May 2026](https://gamemakers.jp/article/2026_05_07_136647/)
- Hit effect build (strike): image board first (warm colours for energy strength, debris and flying sparks as filler). Materials listed: particles, debris, a spike animation, a centre flare, and one more animated element (caption garbled). Steps: emit round particles radially; stretch them long and thin for directionality; high initial speed then deceleration to suggest air resistance; add irregular wobble; make them warm-orange emissive; fling stone debris outward while rotating; place the spike animation radially for impact, plus one more copy "to leave something in the eye"; a flare in the centre as heat source. Then tune in game: randomise angle and lifetime so repeated hits are not monotonous, and add screen shake (and a second screen process, probably blur; caption garbled). — [ASW Academy lecture, part 1, ~03:00 to 05:00](https://youtu.be/iJwbiIa7D3E)
- Slash effect build: see section 4 for the full step list and numbers (3D trajectory mesh, 6 hand-drawn frames, dark underlay, tip gradient). — [ASW Academy lecture, part 1, ~05:20 to 07:45](https://youtu.be/iJwbiIa7D3E)
- Character-specific effect (ice-pillar special; GameMakers identifies the character as Dizzy): from the motion storyboard he lists four effect types needed: generation, sustain, destruction, follow-up. Meshes: ice base, pillar body, two kinds of shatter debris. Textures: shading information plus ice texture and gloss, not over-detailed because the engine shader finishes it. In engine: reflection map for gloss, the ice texture, a detail map; outlines are added to base and pillar; a glint animation over the whole ice; small particles around it; cold mist. — [ASW Academy lecture, part 2, ~00:10 to 02:30](https://youtu.be/cfPC4ptxYgU); [GameMakers, 7 May 2026](https://gamemakers.jp/article/2026_05_07_136647/)
- Stepped mesh growth: the pillar's generation "plays back, in succession, pillar models grown in stages"; the follow-up where it sinks into the ground plays the generation models in reverse; the ice floor spreads by scaling. Warning given: "if you use scaling for every element it looks cheap". — [ASW Academy lecture, part 2, ~02:30 to 03:20](https://youtu.be/cfPC4ptxYgU)
- Simulation is trimmed, not used whole: shatter was tested with a destruction simulation, but "reproducing all of the simulation result is too realistic and lasts too long", so only part is used; the destruction part "has no hitbox, so its saturation is lowered" (caption reads サイド, almost certainly 彩度). — [ASW Academy lecture, part 2, ~01:20 and ~02:55](https://youtu.be/cfPC4ptxYgU)
- Fluid (tap water in a cutscene): simulated in software, only the looping high-volume part kept, imported as a mesh sequence, thickened by pushing vertices along normals in the vertex shader, plus a scrolling noise map; stated trade-off: works from any camera angle, but more frames mean more polygons and heavier data. — [ASW Academy lecture, part 2, ~04:20 to 06:15](https://youtu.be/cfPC4ptxYgU)
- Partial transparency on meshes (e.g. an opponent sealed in ice) is done with vertex alpha on the 3D model. — [ASW Academy lecture, part 2, ~03:40](https://youtu.be/cfPC4ptxYgU)

Engines and company-level statements

### Inferences
- Strive's pipeline is best summarised as "3D mesh carries the shape and range, hand-drawn texture frames carry the anime look, material tricks (dither, VAT, POM) solve sorting and camera problems". That is my synthesis of Ogawa's two talks, not a sentence he says.
- Ogawa refers to registering draw-order values as a "dynamic parameter" in the material and animating them over time in the "effect editor". "Dynamic Parameter" is the name of a module in Unreal Engine 4's Cascade particle editor, so Strive's effects were probably authored in Cascade. He never names Cascade or Niagara.
- "1 drawing per 4 frames" (Strive cutscene overlays) and "15 pictures per second" (Xrd and DBFZ cutscenes) are the same rate at 60 fps, so the hand-drawn effect overlays are locked to the character animation step.

### Gaps
- Dragon Ball FighterZ: I found no developer statement on how beams, auras, explosions or hit sparks are built. The two official dragonball.news developer features (Feb 2018) were unreachable and may contain some.
- Granblue Fantasy Versus / Rising: no talk, article or interview on effect construction found; only the readability remark above.
- No source names the DCC, paint or simulation software used for Strive effects (the talks say only "DCC tool", "drawing software", "video-editing software"). Softimage is named only for Xrd character and cutscene work.
- No statement found on Niagara or Unreal Engine 5 in any ASW effects source.

## 3. Character VFX identity and readability rules

### Takeaway
ASW's stated rules are about readability rather than palette: the effect must match the hitbox exactly, effects are on screen very briefly, large shapes go behind the characters and small sparks in front, and the centre of a hit is kept modest with the impact pushed outward. Identity is described through matching the effect to the attack type and to the character's motion, and through a Strive rule that each new character brings one new kind of expression; I found no ASW statement defining per-character colour palettes or shape motifs.

### Cited Findings
- PRIMARY (Ogawa, 2026 lecture). Two jobs of effects: (1) feedback and exhilaration when a hit lands (sparks, screen shake, destruction); (2) "making information visible": trajectory of a sword or bullet shows range and direction, a red signal warns of a big attack, and colour rules identify attributes (his generic example: fire red, healing green). — [ASW Academy lecture, part 1, ~00:40 to 02:00](https://youtu.be/iJwbiIa7D3E)
- PRIMARY (Ogawa). Fighting-game specifics: "the tip of the effect that appears when a sword is swung is made to match strictly the range where the attack hits"; hitboxes are stricter than in other genres; active time is short and set per frame, combos chain constantly, "so effect display time is very short". — [ASW Academy lecture, part 1, ~02:05 to 02:50](https://youtu.be/iJwbiIa7D3E)
- PRIMARY (Ogawa). Fairness: "because the view is basically from the side, if effects are too flashy and the character's movement cannot be seen, it becomes an unfair game. So large effects are placed behind the characters and fine effects in front." — [ASW Academy lecture, part 1, ~02:50](https://youtu.be/iJwbiIa7D3E); same point in [GameMakers, 7 May 2026](https://gamemakers.jp/article/2026_05_07_136647/)
- PRIMARY (Ogawa, 2021 talk). Strive's guideline was "secure character visibility while also expressing visual impact", so draw priority got more attention than in the previous game. In the previous game the main animation of a hit effect was drawn in front of the character; in Strive it is drawn behind the character, or "the centre of the hit is kept modest and wind-pressure expression is added around it for impact". Flashy special moves are tuned so the character's motion stays recognisable. — [ASW Academy, Strive effects Part 1, ~01:10 to 01:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vha6dkrVvZ0)
- PRIMARY (Ogawa, 2021 talk). Not everything goes to the back: "the largest part goes behind and the other filler elements in front", giving fine priority differences and depth. Hit effects are divided into strike and slash types. — [ASW Academy, Strive effects Part 1, ~01:30 to 02:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vha6dkrVvZ0); [GameMakers, 10 Oct 2025](https://gamemakers.jp/article/2025_10_10_120081/)
- PRIMARY (Ogawa, 2021 talk). Draw priority can change over time: Zato's shadow dragon starts behind the character and moves in front after the attack, by animating a material parameter in the effect editor; used "when we want the effect's animation shown properly to the end". — [ASW Academy, Strive effects Part 1, ~02:00 to 02:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vha6dkrVvZ0)
- PRIMARY (Ogawa, 2021 talk). Ground-contact effects: uneven terrain and grass interfere with the shadow effect under a character, so the shadow is drawn with front priority and a material mask is applied against the character only; the same mask is used in cutscenes. — [ASW Academy, Strive effects Part 1, ~02:55 to 03:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vha6dkrVvZ0)
- PRIMARY (Ogawa, 2026 lecture). Bright stages: a dark underlay is put under slash trails "to prevent visibility dropping on bright stages"; ice effects are checked "on various stages" for colour balance and contact with the ground. — [ASW Academy lecture, part 1, ~06:20](https://youtu.be/iJwbiIa7D3E); [part 2, ~03:30](https://youtu.be/cfPC4ptxYgU)
- PRIMARY (Ogawa, 2026 lecture). Matching effect to attack: "what matters in hit effects is to match the content of the attack": fist, sword, heavy firearm, and the size of the damage each get a different hit-effect type; the effect's angle follows the character's movement to keep directionality. — [ASW Academy lecture, part 1, ~04:55 to 05:20](https://youtu.be/iJwbiIa7D3E)
- PRIMARY (Ogawa, 2026 lecture). Hitbox state is shown in the look: parts with no hitbox are desaturated, the second half of a slash should "look like it has no hitbox", and ice changes colour or shatters according to hitbox presence, "finally turning it into a rule of the game". — [ASW Academy lecture, part 2, ~02:55 and ~04:00](https://youtu.be/cfPC4ptxYgU)
- PRIMARY (Ogawa, 2026 lecture). His closing three criteria: the situation must be understood in an instant; do not get in the way of play; make actions feel good. "If this balance breaks, however pretty the graphics, it cannot be called a good effect." Final tuning happens after implementation, checking visibility, draw cost and play feel with people actually playing. — [ASW Academy lecture, part 2, ~06:20 to 06:55](https://youtu.be/cfPC4ptxYgU)
- PRIMARY (Sakamura). Strive's "one new expression per new character" rule explicitly counts effects as one of the places the novelty can go. — [CGWORLD, ASW feature](https://cgworld.jp/special-feature/202508-asw.html)
- SECONDARY with developer statement (Xrd). Emissive effects do not light the scene and pure effects are unlit, so an effect looks the same in every condition; strong bloom/diffusion is kept for cutscenes. — [4Gamer, Xrd graphics part 2](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140714079/)
- PRIMARY (Sekine, GBVS). Too much staging makes the game hard to see; balancing visibility and coolness was hard. — [Famitsu, GBVS graphics interview](https://www.famitsu.com/news/202002/10191907.html)
- Snippet only: in a 2020 Play UK piece Ishiwatari is reported as saying Strive's major focus is that "even casual spectators can easily understand what is happening on screen". I did not open the article. — [PressReader, Play UK, 11 Mar 2020](https://www.pressreader.com/australia/play-uk-1/20200311/281590947613682)

### Inferences
- Character identity in ASW's own words comes from motif-driven, character-specific assets (ice pillars for the ice character, shadow dragon for Zato) built per move from the motion storyboard, not from a stated palette system. Palette-per-character is visible in the games but I found no developer statement about it.
- The readability rules form a consistent hierarchy: hitbox truth first, character silhouette second, spectacle third. This ordering is my reading of the quotes above.

### Gaps
- No ASW statement found on effect brightness values relative to characters, on a maximum hit-effect size, or on a fixed on-screen duration.
- No statement found on colour palette or shape language assigned per character, or on how player-1/player-2 colour variants affect effects (only that hand-drawn overlay colour is sometimes swapped per character via a data table).

## 4. Concrete parameters found (frames, counts, sizes, slash-trail construction)

### Takeaway
The only fully specified effect recipe is Ogawa's Strive slash: a mesh modelled along the weapon's path sized to the hitbox, a dark underlay, a 6-frame hand-drawn animation texture that moves forward while fading, a gradient glow on the leading edge, and playback speed tuned so hitbox-active and hitbox-inactive phases look different. Other hard numbers are sparse: 15 drawings per second for cutscene-rate animation (1 drawing per 4 frames), per-pose holds of 1 to 4 frames in battle, about 1,000 effects and about 20 post-process filters in Strive's story mode.

### Cited Findings
- Slash trail, step by step (PRIMARY, Ogawa 2026): (1) image board drawn with trail length matched to hitbox size, because "the hitbox has priority"; (2) "this time the blade's path is expressed with a 3D model, so the path itself is modelled", with the part touching the blade modelled at higher precision so it lines up; (3) texture applied to check the look; (4) the slash animation is drawn: "the slash's active time is short but we want to show the afterglow properly, so this time it is 6 drawings"; (5) to give the blade momentum the drawing "advances forward while disappearing"; (6) "the second half should look as if it has no hitbox". — [ASW Academy lecture, part 1, ~05:20 to 06:20](https://youtu.be/iJwbiIa7D3E)
- Slash trail, assembly in the editor (PRIMARY, Ogawa 2026): a dark underlay on the path mesh (for bright stages); the slash animation texture on top; a gradient glow along the leading edge "to emphasise the sharpness of the tip"; then playback speed is adjusted in game "so that a visual difference appears depending on whether the hitbox exists" (the video marks hitbox-active frames with a red box and inactive with blue); checked together with the slash hit effect and the opponent's reaction "as a set". — [ASW Academy lecture, part 1, ~06:20 to 07:20](https://youtu.be/iJwbiIa7D3E); pipeline summarised in [GameMakers, 7 May 2026](https://gamemakers.jp/article/2026_05_07_136647/)
- Slash variety (PRIMARY, Ogawa 2026): one character has several slash attacks, so trail shapes and variations are increased to avoid a monotonous picture. No count given. — [ASW Academy lecture, part 1, ~07:35](https://youtu.be/iJwbiIa7D3E)
- Xrd weapon smear (PRIMARY, Motomura 2015): an extra mesh of the smear shape is added as an effect and "only lasts for like a frame on the screen"; or the sword mesh itself is swapped for a stretched one. — [GDC 2015 talk recording, ~49:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yhGjCzxJV3E)
- Hit effect variation (PRIMARY, Ogawa 2026): angle and lifetime are given "moderate randomness" so consecutive hits do not look identical; hit-effect type changes with attack type and damage size. No count of variants given. — [ASW Academy lecture, part 1, ~04:40 to 05:10](https://youtu.be/iJwbiIa7D3E)
- Frame rates (PRIMARY): Xrd cutscenes 15 pictures/s (Sakamura); DBFZ cutscenes a uniform 15 pictures/s (Motomura); Strive cutscene hand-drawn effects 1 drawing per 4 frames with backgrounds at full frame rate (Ogawa). — [4Gamer, Xrd part 2](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140714079/); [4Gamer, DBFZ interview](https://www.4gamer.net/games/384/G038402/20171211079/); [ASW Academy, Strive effects Part 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vha6dkrVvZ0)
- Battle pose holds (PRIMARY, Sakamura): example sequence "2F, 3F, 3F, 1F, 1F, 2F, 2F, 3F, 4F" at 60 fps; a 2F pose is on screen 33.33 ms. Storyboard example: 20 drawings for 54 frames. Projectile and jump-arc movement update at 60 fps. — [4Gamer, Xrd part 2](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140714079/)
- Reference rates quoted by ASW for anime (PRIMARY, Motomura 2015): characters at 8 or 12 per second, backgrounds/camera at 24. — [GDC 2015 talk recording, ~43:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yhGjCzxJV3E)
- Volume (PRIMARY, Ogawa 2021): about 1,000 effects including variants for Strive; about 20 post-process filters with at most 4 parameters each; a cap of 50 placed assets per story scene; story mode about 4.5 hours. — [ASW Academy, Strive effects Part 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nTZM-1SDAp4)
- Render target (SECONDARY, Xrd): 64-bit buffer, 16-bit float per ARGB channel, HDR, with bloom on over-bright values. — [4Gamer, Xrd part 2](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140714079/)
- Projection (SECONDARY, Xrd; relevant to how effects line up with 2D hitboxes): about 30% perspective and 70% orthographic blended horizontally; the attacker is drawn with a depth offset of about 1 metre toward the camera so it always passes the depth test. — [4Gamer, Xrd part 2](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140714079/)
- Hitstop, Marvel Tōkon (PRIMARY, Sekine, May 2026): after beta feedback "the recovery of tag and cancel, and the hitstop of wall hits, were shortened ... about half compared with the initial version", to speed up tempo. No frame count given. — [4Gamer, Tōkon interview, 11 May 2026](https://www.4gamer.net/games/916/G091625/20260511024/)

### Inferences
- A 6-drawing slash at the cutscene step of 4 frames would last 24 frames, but Ogawa says playback speed is tuned per attack to the hitbox timing, so the real on-screen duration is not derivable from the talk.
- The talk shows no UV-scroll or alpha-erosion step for slashes: the fade is drawn into the 6 frames by hand ("advances forward while disappearing"). Whether an erosion/dissolve material is also used cannot be told from the narration.

### Gaps
- No source gives effect texture resolutions, flipbook grid sizes, polygon counts for effect meshes, or the number of hit-spark variants in any of the five games.
- No source gives hitstop frame counts for Xrd, Strive, DBFZ or GBVS, or screen-shake amplitudes.
- The 2026 lecture's on-screen slides (image boards, texture sheets, the red/blue hitbox overlay) were not viewable to me, only the narration; they likely show sizes and frame layouts worth checking by eye.

## 5. Marvel Tōkon: Fighting Souls: what is published about its effects or visual direction

### Takeaway
As of November 2026 I found no technical talk, article or developer breakdown of Tōkon's effects. Published developer statements are about direction only: match-ending moves are framed like a manga panel, a beam of light travels between characters on tag (the "Soul" of the title), the look fuses American comics with Japanese shōnen manga, and Marvel asked ASW to use its own anime style.

### Cited Findings
- PRIMARY (Sekine, game director, Oct 2025). "When the match is decided by a stage change or a Super Skill, it becomes like a single scene from a manga, a staging different from before." The character-select UI is "a new challenge fusing American comics and Japanese shōnen manga". — [PlayStation Blog JP interview](https://blog.ja.playstation.com/2025/10/03/20251003-mtfs/); English version: [PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2025/10/03/marvel-tokon-fighting-souls-interview/)
- PRIMARY (Takeshi Yamanaka, producer). "There is a staging where light moves when you switch the character you control, and we thought of that light as the 'Soul'", which is where "Fighting Souls" (and its Japanese form "Tōkon") comes from. — [PlayStation Blog JP interview](https://blog.ja.playstation.com/2025/10/03/20251003-mtfs/)
- PRIMARY (Sekine, 4Gamer, May 2026). Episode Mode uses motion comics: manga artists draw the base manga and "movement is added panel by panel"; five teams, close to 10 hours. — [4Gamer, Tōkon interview](https://www.4gamer.net/games/916/G091625/20260511024/)
- PRIMARY (ASW staff in the official making-of video, July 2026). Japanese and overseas artists drew the story art, some "manga-like", some "American-comic-like". The 17-minute video's ASW segments cover character design, story, stages and music; it contains no statement about effects. — [Official documentary video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J2150xwHY1U); reported by [Push Square, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2026/07/from-japan-to-the-world-arcsys-reveals-how-it-built-the-unique-style-of-ps5s-new-marvel-fighter)
- PRIMARY (4Gamer, May 2026). On design direction Marvel told ASW "of course we want you to bring out the Arc System Works character". — [4Gamer, Tōkon interview](https://www.4gamer.net/games/916/G091625/20260511024/)
- PRIMARY (Den Faminico Gamer interview, May 2026). The developers list "looks clean but the screen is chaos" among the opposing demands they had to reconcile, and say move staging was given "real Japanese anime, Arc-like" treatment. — [Den Faminico Gamer](https://news.denfaminicogamer.jp/interview/260503d)
- Snippet only, not verified by me: outlets report Yamanaka saying an early Western-comic-style shader was tested and that Marvel steered the team to ASW's own look. — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/marvel-tokon-fighting-souls-anime-look-wasnt-pushed-by-arc-system-works-but-marvel-games/); [doope](https://doope.jp/2025/06156551.html)
- Hitstop tuning statement: see section 4. — [4Gamer, Tōkon interview](https://www.4gamer.net/games/916/G091625/20260511024/)

### Inferences
- The "manga panel" finish is the only developer-described effect-like device specific to Tōkon; how it is rendered (post-process, pre-drawn art, or real-time panels) is not stated.

### Gaps
- No ASW or Sony source found describing halftone afterimages, swing trails, hit sparks or any other battle effect in Tōkon. If the game shows halftone or comic-dot treatments, that is observable in footage but I found no developer statement about it.
- The engine and effect tools used for Tōkon are not confirmed in any source I read (no mention of Unreal Engine 5 or Niagara found).
- No post-release (after August 2026) CEDEC, Unreal Fest or CGWORLD write-up on Tōkon surfaced in my searches; one may exist that search did not return.
