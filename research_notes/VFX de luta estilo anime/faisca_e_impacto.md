# Hit sparks and impact effects: how professional 2D FX animators and fighting-game VFX artists design them

Reading notes for the report writer:

- All frame counts are quoted in the unit the source uses. Fighting-game sources use 60 fps frames ("ticks" in MUGEN; 1 frame = 16.7 ms). Joseph Gilland's book uses film frames at 24 fps (1 frame = 41.7 ms). Conversions are marked as mine.
- Source quality tags used below: **[DEV]** = statement by the developer or a working professional; **[BOOK]** = professional textbook; **[COMMUNITY DATA]** = frame data compiled by the fighting-game community from the games; **[FAN]** = fan analysis or opinion; **[SECONDARY]** = a summary of a primary source I could not open.
- Several primary pages could not be opened from this environment (Dustloop, SuperCombo wiki, Mizuumi wiki, TV Tropes, Virtua Fighter forum all returned 403 or a bot wall; the Riot VFX style-guide page and PDF now redirect to the League of Legends home page). Where a number comes only from a search-result snippet of such a page, it is marked "snippet only, not opened".
- The Gilland quotes come from the archive.org OCR text of Volume II, read through an automatic extraction step. Page numbers and wording should be checked against the printed book before being quoted verbatim in a deliverable.

## 1. Anatomy of a hit effect: layers, order, and how long each lasts

### Takeaway
Professional breakdowns agree on a small set of layers that fire almost together but offset by a few frames: a 2-3 frame flash, a fast-expanding core or burst, a ring or shockwave, radial streaks or sparks that outlive the burst slightly, and an optional smoke residual; stronger hits add a layer behind the victim instead of changing the recipe. The only fully numeric fighting-game spark I found (a community Capcom-vs-SNK-style implementation) lasts 16 frames at 60 fps in total, with the main burst gone by frame 10 and the impact flash layer gone by frame 8.

### Cited Findings
- **[DEV-level artist breakdown]** A stylized explosion built by VFX artist "Atif" (Overwatch-inspired) has 18 layers in six groups: shockwave, smoke, instant flash, burst core, burst ring, sparks. Order: the shockwave fires first and "acts as a background light filler for the flash"; dark smoke starts early at the back and lasts to the end; "the instant flash literally lasts about 2-3 frames"; burst core and sparks start together from the centre; the burst ring follows outward while dissolving; sparks "die slightly after the Burst Ring". Layer start times differ by only 2-3 frames, and the author says those small offsets had to be tuned over many iterations because most layers are short-lived. The frame rate is not stated. — [realtimevfx.com, Stylized Frag Launcher Explosion breakdown](https://realtimevfx.com/t/stylized-frag-launcher-explosion-overwatch-inspiration-breakdown-posted/1894?page=2)
- **[DEV feedback]** On a stylised hit spark posted for critique, Riot VFX artist "Beardilocks" praised the sequence "white flash, into the black flash, into the ramp down timing of the rest of the elements", the directional variety given by cross-shaped pieces, and the hue range white, yellow, orange, red. He criticised the ending for clipping off abruptly (fix: fade the middle to black and fade opacity), and the detail for being too high-frequency and noisy (fix: reduce detail frequency in one element, or keep the outer ring in a narrower value range). The spark's listed layers were: explosion core, pillars shooting out at the sides, a fire ring, cross pieces shooting straight out, and an outer ring that spins out at the end. In version 2 the author moved the fire layer to the back so it supports the core instead of covering it. — [realtimevfx.com, Stylised Explosive and Fiery Hit-spark](https://realtimevfx.com/t/stylised-explosive-and-fiery-hit-spark-looking-for-feedback/11286)
- **[FAN / community implementation, Capcom-vs-SNK style, 60 ticks per second]** The MUGEN creator P.o.t.S documents his hit sparks as four stacked parts: a main body, a "center" layer, a "streaks" layer and an "impact" layer, plus a fifth "P2 back" layer only for special and super hits. Numbers: total lifetime 16 ticks (267 ms, my conversion); the main body shrinks linearly to zero scale at tick 10 (167 ms); the centre and streaks grow slightly over the 16 ticks; the impact layer is removed at tick 8 (133 ms) and fades linearly (alpha 128 minus 16 per tick for normal hits); the impact layer is drawn behind the centre and streaks (sprite priority 3 versus 5); the "P2 back" layer is drawn behind everything (priority -3) and fades in 8 ticks (alpha 256 minus 32 per tick). — [Mugen Fighters Guild, P.o.t.S creation diary part 6](https://mugenguild.com/forum/topics/diary-creation-p-o-t-s-character-part-6-santas-little-helpers-191095.0.html)
- **[FAN / community implementation]** In the same source the guard (block) spark is a single element: 8 ticks total (133 ms), fully opaque for ticks 0-3, then alpha drops by 64 per tick from tick 4, while the scale grows every tick. — [Mugen Fighters Guild, P.o.t.S creation diary part 6](https://mugenguild.com/forum/topics/diary-creation-p-o-t-s-character-part-6-santas-little-helpers-191095.0.html)
- MUGEN timing unit: animation durations are in game ticks, 60 ticks per second at normal speed. — [Elecbyte MUGEN docs, AIR format](https://elecbyte.com/mugendocs/air.html)
- **[BOOK, 24 fps film frames]** Gilland's worked explosion example: frame 1 is the initial burst ("The first frame of an explosion looks exactly the same, regardless of the element that is involved"); in frame 2 "the explosion actually shrinks for one frame before exploding with force"; "in frames 3 to 6 we see the initial violent outward force of the explosion"; "in frames 7 and 8, there is a negative frame followed by a black-white frame"; the full sequence "is actually over 80 frames long", condensed to 24 frames for the book by cutting resolving frames. — [Joseph Gilland, Elemental Magic Vol. II, ch. "Blowing It Up", pp. 248, 266-267 (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt)
- **[BOOK]** Impact has two phases even in liquids: a primary splash ("the thin sheet or spray ... that shoots up and out around the object") and a secondary splash caused by the hole the object made, which comes after "a beat, or a pause of several frames, perhaps one half of a second". Either can dominate. — [Gilland, Elemental Magic Vol. II, p. 107 (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt)
- **[DEV]** Capcom's own effect structure for Street Fighter 6: one technique's effect is split into several assets by stage. Ryu's Hadoken uses separate assets for buildup, launch, flight and impact; Shin Shoryuken has a dedicated camera-following "jaw hit in front of the camera" element and a separate foot-on-ground impact element. Each particle's life is authored as Appear, Keep, Vanish intervals that drive alpha. — [Capcom R&D, RE:2023 "Editor Functions and Workflows that Support High-Quality Effects" slides](https://www.docswell.com/s/CAPCOM_RandD/538NVE-RE2023)
- **[DEV]** VFX Apprentice's small-FX course splits a hand-drawn effect into two components animated separately: the primary "burst and flash frame element" first, then the smoke, with a dedicated dissipation pass after the build-up. — [VFX Apprentice, Animating Small 2D FX](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/courses/animating-small-2d-fx)
- **[FAN]** Hit sparks mark the exact point of contact and support hitstop; they do not replace it. — [Critpoints, "Hitstop/Hitfreeze/Hitlag/Hitpause"](https://critpoints.net/2017/05/17/hitstophitfreezehitlaghitpausehitshit/)
- **[Anime]** Screen-space layer: impact frames are "special frames drawn by animators that appear for a split second, often added to make an impact more pronounced" (Japanese term: ショックコマ, "shock frames"). — [Sakugabooru wiki, impact_frames](https://www.sakugabooru.com/wiki/show?title=impact_frames)

### Inferences
- A workable layer list for a hit spark, in firing order, combining the sources above: (1) flash at the contact point, 2-3 frames; (2) main burst or star that expands at once and is gone by about frame 10 at 60 fps; (3) ring or shockwave, starting 0-3 frames after the flash; (4) radial streaks and sparks that outlive the burst by a few frames (total about 16 frames); (5) optional smoke or dust that starts early behind everything and is the last thing to disappear; (6) for specials, supers and counters only, a back layer behind the victim and screen-space elements (impact frame, background flash).
- The "negative frame then black-white frame" in Gilland and the "white flash into black flash" praised by the Riot artist are the same device: a one-frame value inversion right after the brightest frame. It is the hand-drawn ancestor of the anime impact frame.
- In Gilland's example the inversion frames come after the violent expansion (film frames 7-8, about 250-330 ms from the start, my conversion), whereas in game hit sparks the flash is the first thing shown. For a game hit, the flash-first order is the one supported by the game-side sources.
- Strength is added by stacking layers (the "P2 back" layer for specials and supers), which keeps weak and strong hits visibly related.

### Gaps
- I found no official Capcom, Arc System Works, SNK or Lab Zero document that lists the layers and frame counts of their hit sparks. The only layer-by-layer numbers come from a community re-creation (P.o.t.S) and from non-fighting-game VFX breakdowns.
- No source gave durations for a smoke residual on a hit spark, or frame counts for background flashes and impact frames in fighting games.
- Frame-by-frame counts of the original Street Fighter III, Guilty Gear or KOF spark sprites (number of drawings per spark) were not found in any source I could open; they would have to be measured from sprite rips or captured footage.

## 2. Timing and spacing: the pop, easing, number of drawings, total length, and the link with hitstop

### Takeaway
Every professional source describes the same curve: little or no anticipation (at most one frame of "implode"), an almost instant peak in the first few frames, then a much longer, eased dissipation; the peak should sit early in the effect's life and the effect should end sooner than feels natural. In fighting games the spark plays while both characters are frozen in hitstop, which runs roughly 8-15 frames at 60 fps depending on strength and game, so a 10-16 frame spark is sized to fit inside or just beyond the freeze.

### Cited Findings

**The curve**
- **[BOOK]** An explosion is "a rapid and abrupt outward expansion of energy"; its dynamics are "an extremely rapid outward expansion, followed by an acute slowing down of the outward action". Gilland advises pushing the expansion "very quickly into the slower final phase". — [Gilland, Elemental Magic Vol. II, pp. 243, 272 (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt)
- **[BOOK]** One-frame anticipation: "in the second frame or two, we can actually implode the image slightly"; in his example the explosion "shrinks for one frame before exploding with force". — [Gilland, Elemental Magic Vol. II, pp. 266-267 (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt)
- **[BOOK, 24 fps]** Explosions grow "over a number of frames, anywhere from 3 to 30, depending on the size and scope of the explosion" (125 ms to 1.25 s, my conversion). For a mushroom cloud "there is actually only one frame of the initial explosion". — [Gilland, Elemental Magic Vol. II, pp. 265, 275 (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt)
- **[BOOK]** Overlap: a secondary explosion can shoot out quickly "while the initial explosion is slowing down". — [Gilland, Elemental Magic Vol. II, p. 273 (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt)
- **[SECONDARY notes on Gilland Vol. I]** "Whip and wave": effects start fast and angular, then become soft and slow. Overlapping and offsetting the timing of parts makes the animation more dynamic. — [Student notes on Elemental Magic](https://amystevenshonours.wordpress.com/2014/10/06/elemental-magic/)
- **[SECONDARY review]** Gilland's core idea: a splash, flame or explosion starts with a burst of energy that gradually runs out; thinking of it this way makes timing easier. — [animationbooks.tv review of Elemental Magic Vol. I](https://animationbooks.tv/2016/03/20/elemental-magic-volume-i/)
- **[DEV, via SECONDARY]** Riot's League of Legends VFX style guide, as summarised by VFX Apprentice: linear timing is to be avoided; an effect has three stages, a brief anticipation/cast, an impact/pop at peak value and saturation, and a smooth dissipation. The summary places the peak within the first quarter of the effect's life. Its tenth tip is titled "If your FX feel long, they're waaaaay too long". I could not open Riot's original PDF to confirm the "first quarter" figure. — [VFX Apprentice, 10 Design Tips from the LoL VFX Style Guide](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips); original guide location (now redirecting): [Riot Nexus, VFX Style Guide](https://nexus.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/2017/10/dev-leagues-vfx-style-guide/)
- **[DEV]** VFX Apprentice's timing vocabulary: anticipation (wind-up), climax (the pop), dissipation (fall-off). "Instant (impulse) effects skip anticipation and open at high intensity." Advice for the fall-off: reduce the intensity of some elements before the effect is fully gone, to give "a pleasant outro". Plan timing on a simple block-in before drawing detail. In fighting games the effect must match the damage precisely for "competitive integrity". — [VFX Apprentice, "The Soul of Effects: What is Timing in VFX?"](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/the-soul-of-effects-what-is-timing-in-vfx)
- **[DEV feedback]** An effect that "clips off abruptly" at the end reads as a fault even when the pop is good. — [realtimevfx.com, Stylised Hit-spark thread](https://realtimevfx.com/t/stylised-explosive-and-fiery-hit-spark-looking-for-feedback/11286)
- **[FAN speculation]** A realtimevfx poster guessed that fitting effects to fighting-game timings and hitboxes "would encourage punchy timing and put more weight on an effect's fall-off than its build-up". Nobody replied to confirm it. — [realtimevfx.com, VFX in Fighting Games](https://realtimevfx.com/t/vfx-in-fighting-games/6599)

**Numbers for a game hit spark (60 fps)**
- **[FAN / community implementation]** P.o.t.S hit spark: 16 ticks total for every strength (weak, medium, strong, special, super); main body shrinks linearly from full to zero over ticks 0-9; impact flash layer 8 ticks. Guard spark: 8 ticks. The spark is scaled and faded procedurally from one or two drawings, with a random start angle (30 degrees times a random number from 0 to 11) and a 30-degree rotation per tick, instead of a long hand-drawn sequence. — [Mugen Fighters Guild, P.o.t.S creation diary part 6](https://mugenguild.com/forum/topics/diary-creation-p-o-t-s-character-part-6-santas-little-helpers-191095.0.html)
- **[FAN / community implementation]** The sparks are flagged to ignore hitpause: they keep animating while the two characters are frozen. — [Mugen Fighters Guild, P.o.t.S creation diary part 6](https://mugenguild.com/forum/topics/diary-creation-p-o-t-s-character-part-6-santas-little-helpers-191095.0.html)
- **[DEV-level artist breakdown]** Instant flash: "about 2-3 frames". — [realtimevfx.com, Frag Launcher breakdown](https://realtimevfx.com/t/stylized-frag-launcher-explosion-overwatch-inspiration-breakdown-posted/1894?page=2)
- **[Low-authority game-dev articles, unverified tuning values]** White flash on the hit target for 2 frames (4 frames for a critical hit); a "bright white flash for 1-2 frames" on a sword clash. — [freegamesprites.com, game feel 2D combat juice tricks](https://freegamesprites.com/es/news/game-feel-2d-combat-juice-tricks)

**Hitstop values (60 fps frames)**
- **[FAN expert analysis: Maj, Sonic Hurricane]** "In most games, light attacks freeze around 9 frames, medium attacks around 11, hard attacks around 13." "In the oldschool SF2 series they were all around 14 frames regardless of button strength." Street Fighter IV examples: a connected standing jab about 10 frames; Vega crouching light punch 8 frames; C. Viper crouching heavy punch a "12-frame impact freeze window"; projectiles listed at "Hit stop: 8F" in the SFIV Master Guide (reported by a commenter). "Impact freeze is generally the same regardless of whether the attack hits or gets blocked." A projectile freezes only the victim (about 12 frames), not the attacker. Ultimate Mortal Kombat 3 has no impact freeze at all. — [Sonic Hurricane, "Impact Freeze"](https://sonichurricane.com/?p=1043)
- **[COMMUNITY DATA, snippet only, not opened]** Guilty Gear Xrd attack levels 0 to 4 have hitstop 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 frames. On counter hit the extra hitstop applies only to the receiver. Guilty Gear XX uses levels 0-5, Xrd levels 0-4. — [Dustloop, GGXRD Attack Attributes](https://dustloop.com/w/GGXRD/Attack_Attributes); [Dustloop, Dizzy frame data](https://www.dustloop.com/w/GGXRD-R2/Dizzy/Frame_Data); [Dustloop, Elphelt frame data](https://www.dustloop.com/w/GGXRD-R2/Elphelt_Valentine/Frame_Data); [Guilty Gear fandom wiki, Offense](https://guiltygear.fandom.com/wiki/Offense)
- **[COMMUNITY DATA, snippet only, not opened]** King of Fighters XIII: light attacks 7 frames of hitstop, heavy attacks 11. — [SuperCombo wiki mirror, KOF XIII Hitstun/Blockstun state](https://srk.shib.live/w/The_King_of_Fighters_XIII/Character_States/Hitstun_Blockstun_state)
- **[COMMUNITY DATA, snippet only, not opened]** Street Fighter 6: "Heavier attacks have longer hitstop, and some Punish Counters with special effects can have extra long hitstop." No numbers retrieved. — [SuperCombo wiki mirror, SF6 Game Data](https://srk.shib.live/w/Street_Fighter_6/Game_Data)
- **[DEV: Masahiro Sakurai]** "When you strike the opponent, both parties momentarily freeze, emphasizing the power of impact." Both fighters freeze for the same time; stronger attacks give longer hitstop; projectiles have less; electric attacks automatically get more; there is a hard cap. Ryu "still has less hitstop in Smash than he did in Street Fighter II". During the freeze the victim (and slightly the attacker) vibrates, side to side on the ground and up and down in the air, with amplitude shrinking over the freeze; hurtboxes stay still. "During hitstop, we take four frames to smoothly transition from the initial flinch to the hurt animation." Sakurai would like longer hitstop but holds back because a frozen pair is open to a third player. — [Source Gaming translation of Sakurai's Famitsu column vol. 490](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/)
- **[COMMUNITY DATA]** Smash hitlag formulas (d = damage): Melee floor(d/3 + 3); Brawl and Smash 4 floor(d x 0.3846154 + 5); Ultimate floor(d x 0.65 + 6); electric multiplier 1.5; caps 20 frames (Melee) and 30 frames (Brawl onward). A 15% hit gives 8 frames in Melee, 10 in Brawl/Smash 4, 15 in Ultimate. — [SmashWiki, Hitlag](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Hitlag)
- **[FAN]** In Street Fighter II, hitstop "like 10 frames" also extends the cancel window; without hitstop (the article names Dark Souls 2) hits feel weak and are harder to read. — [Critpoints, hitstop article](https://critpoints.net/2017/05/17/hitstophitfreezehitlaghitpausehitshit/); note the disagreement with Sonic Hurricane's "around 14 frames" for the SF2 series: [Sonic Hurricane](https://sonichurricane.com/?p=1043)
- **[SECONDARY, unverified]** Non-fighting action games: hit pause "typically 2 to 5 frames". Vlambeer's "Art of Screenshake" talk (Jan Willem Nijman, 2013) lists hit pause, screenshake, hit reaction, knockback and "permanence" among its steps, according to a fan re-creation of the talk. — [gamejuice.pages.dev, hit stop frame trick](https://gamejuice.pages.dev/articles/hit-stop-frame-trick); [dkliao, Art of Screenshake recreation devlog](https://dkliao.itch.io/the-art-of-screenshake-recreation/devlog/451576/quick-breakdown-of-all-the-effects)

**Drawing count and playback rate**
- **[BOOK, 24 fps]** Gilland animates effects straight ahead and rough first ("always animate drawing fast, sketching roughly with energy and abandon"); he usually thinks "of each drawing as 1/12 of a second" (on twos at 24 fps), sometimes working on every fourth drawing first. — [Gilland, Elemental Magic Vol. II, p. 153 (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt)
- **[Anime lesson page]** A Ninja Scroll punch used as a teaching example "was animated entirely on twos", with the first drawings as anticipation. — [ART 379 lesson, Anticipation and Impacts](https://sites.google.com/view/art379/lessons/anticipation-impacts)
- **[DEV: Mariel Cartwright, Skullgirls lead animator, via SECONDARY]** A designer may allow "only six frames to deliver a punch"; even one frame of anticipation improves readability; one overshoot frame followed by a pull-back, combined with smears, sells power. — [GDC Vault, Animation Bootcamp: Fluid and Powerful Animation within Frame Restrictions](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020575/Animation-Bootcamp-Fluid-and-Powerful); [80.lv summary](https://80.lv/articles/tips-on-making-fluid-and-powerful-animations)
- **[DEV: pixel artist Raymond Schlitter]** In his punch and kick breakdowns the hit effect starts on the "Hit" frame; the jab's Hit frame is held slightly longer "to enhance the sense of impact"; the heavy round kick adds "delay, screen shake, and a heavier hit effect". Frame lists: jab 5 frames (Smear, Hit, Follow Through, Recover, Overshoot); cross 7; front kick 4; round kick 7. — [Slynyrd Pixelblog 53, Punches and Kicks](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2024/11/25/pixelblog-53-punches-and-kicks)
- **[DEV]** Flipbook practice: sheets usually hold a power-of-two number of frames, and for side-scrollers "like fighting games with larger FX" VFX Apprentice suggests at least 1024 x 1024 for the sheet. — [VFX Apprentice, What are Flipbooks?](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/what-are-flipbooks-in-games)

### Inferences
- Hitstop of roughly 9 / 11 / 13 frames (light / medium / heavy) against a 16-frame spark whose main burst ends at frame 10 means: the burst and flash play out entirely while the characters are frozen, and only the thin tail of streaks is still visible when motion resumes. That is consistent with sparks being set to ignore hitpause.
- Because hitstop is usually the same on hit and on block, the block spark being half as long (8 versus 16 frames in the one implementation with numbers) is a purely visual choice to make a block feel smaller than a hit.
- Converting Gilland to 60 fps: one film frame is 2.5 game frames. His "implode for one frame" is about 2-3 game frames, too long for a light hit; in a game hit spark the anticipation is better delivered by the attack animation itself (smear and one anticipation frame, per Cartwright), with the spark starting directly at its peak, as VFX Apprentice describes for impulse effects.
- If a hand-drawn spark is played on twos at 60 fps (30 drawings per second), a 16-frame spark is about 8 drawings and an 8-frame block spark about 4. This is arithmetic from the durations above, not a figure given by any source.
- Sources do not scale spark duration with strength (P.o.t.S uses 16 ticks for all); they scale size, add layers and lengthen hitstop. A heavy hit therefore feels longer mostly because the freeze is longer.

### Gaps
- No source gave the number of drawings in an official Street Fighter, Guilty Gear, BlazBlue, KOF or Skullgirls hit spark, nor whether they are played on ones, twos or threes.
- Exact per-strength hitstop tables for Street Fighter 6, Skullgirls, BlazBlue, Dragon Ball FighterZ and Marvel vs. Capcom could not be opened (wiki bot walls). Only the Guilty Gear Xrd 11-15 and KOF XIII 7 / 11 figures surfaced, both through search snippets.
- No source documented easing curves numerically for hit sparks; the only explicit curves are the linear scale and alpha ramps in the community implementation.
- The Riot "peak in the first quarter" figure is from a third-party summary and is unconfirmed against the original guide.

## 3. Shape design, direction, size, and colour and value rules

### Takeaway
The stated professional rules are: strong asymmetrical silhouette, no repetition and no parallels, sharper shapes for more dangerous hits, hand-drawn shapes with a mix of hard and soft edges and low-frequency detail, and a value hierarchy in which the brightest values are reserved for the instant of impact while the body of the effect sits in mid values with higher saturation than the scene. In games, strength is conveyed chiefly by size, with documented steps of about 17% per strength level in one implementation.

### Cited Findings

**Shape**
- **[BOOK, via notes]** "We always strive for striking silhouettes in our animation." "Avoiding symmetry and parallels in tone adds depth, weight and volume." Repeated symbols should be avoided; parallel lines rarely occur in nature; effect silhouettes should be asymmetrical. "Stylize, simplify, and find the energy flow!" "Use exaggeration to add drama." Without contrast in shapes and timing, "animation appears stiff and mannered". — [Student notes on Elemental Magic Vol. I](https://amystevenshonours.wordpress.com/2014/10/06/elemental-magic/)
- **[BOOK]** Fire needs "a good silhouette, and the avoidance of too much symmetry or repetition"; "the more irregularity we animate into our fire animation the better". In any design "make sure we include the principle of chaos, or lack of repetition"; holes that break up a sheet "should not appear repetitive or regularly spaced". — [Gilland, Elemental Magic Vol. II, pp. 99, 190, 198 (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt)
- **[BOOK]** Direction comes from the force: a straight-down drop gives "a fairly even and symmetrically shaped splash", while an off-centre trajectory shifts the weight of the splash to one side. Tears and holes "do not ever appear at strange, obtuse angles contrary to the forces propelling" the material. "Driving force for explosions are simple: travels directly outward from fuel source." — [Gilland, Elemental Magic Vol. II, p. 97 (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt); [Student notes on Vol. I](https://amystevenshonours.wordpress.com/2014/10/06/elemental-magic/)
- **[BOOK, via notes]** Fire's overall shape is a triangle, which suggests harshness and violence; the interior shapes show its energy. — [Student notes on Elemental Magic Vol. I](https://amystevenshonours.wordpress.com/2014/10/06/elemental-magic/)
- **[DEV: Riot VFX artist]** High-impact spells should use sharper shapes; damage spells should be "bigger, brighter, and more threatening"; knock-ups should have strong vertical elements; heals should have a non-threatening shape. Each ability should carry its own design language so it is recognised at a glance. "If everything is important, nothing is important." — [realtimevfx.com, Anatomy of a League of Legends missile, part 2](https://realtimevfx.com/t/anatomy-of-a-league-of-legends-missile-part-2-what-are-we-communicating-with-our-vfx/13062)
- **[DEV, via SECONDARY]** Riot style guide: hand-draw and hand-paint shapes, mixing soft and hard edges; avoid photographic textures and noisy procedural detail; do not stack many alpha shapes or contradictory shapes that muddy the silhouette. Every effect needs primary elements (the gameplay signal: clear silhouette, high value, high opacity, a sharp focal point) and secondary elements (flavour: lower value, lower opacity, simpler, blurrier, subtler motion). Motion can be shown by blurring a shape along its travel or by putting the brightest, sharpest contrast on the leading edge. — [VFX Apprentice, 10 Design Tips from the LoL VFX Style Guide](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips)
- **[DEV feedback]** Too much high-frequency detail and high black-to-white contrast in a secondary ring makes its shapes compete with the core; cross-shaped pieces add welcome directional variety. — [realtimevfx.com, Stylised Hit-spark thread](https://realtimevfx.com/t/stylised-explosive-and-fiery-hit-spark-looking-for-feedback/11286)
- **[DEV: Capcom, via SECONDARY]** Street Fighter 6 separates outcomes by silhouette: the hit effect forms an "O", the Drive Parry light forms an "X" ("We actually even made the light form an X, as opposed to the hit effect forming an O"). Conceptually, hits push into the opponent while blocked attacks stop in front of them, and a parry should look deflected, not absorbed. — [EventHubs report on Capcom's SF6 developer column](https://eventhubs.com/news/2023/jul/18/capcom-early-sf6-drive-parry)
- **[DEV: Capcom]** SF6's StretchBlur feature samples the image in the attack area and maps it onto billboards and trails, keeping detail in the attack area that ordinary motion blur would lose; it is used for Chun-Li's Lightning Kick afterimages. — [Capcom R&D RE:2023 slides](https://www.docswell.com/s/CAPCOM_RandD/538NVE-RE2023)
- **[FAN, snippet only, not opened]** Games with several damage types usually give each its own spark, and blocked damage "is nearly always indicated by a softer ripple-like effect". — [TV Tropes, Hit Spark](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/HitSpark)

**Size**
- **[FAN / community implementation]** Base scale of the main spark body by strength: weak 0.30, medium 0.35, strong 0.40, special 0.45, super 0.50 (all multiplied by 0.65). Relative to weak: medium 1.17x, strong 1.33x, special 1.5x, super 1.67x (ratios are my arithmetic). "The only difference between weak, medium and strong hitsparks are the scales"; some authors recolour sparks for specials and supers while "classic" authors only resize. — [Mugen Fighters Guild, P.o.t.S creation diary part 6](https://mugenguild.com/forum/topics/diary-creation-p-o-t-s-character-part-6-santas-little-helpers-191095.0.html)
- **[FAN, snippet only, not opened]** "The bigger the spark, the bigger the damage"; a hit spark indicates the amount of damage, while a separate "hit effect" indicates the type of hit. — [Virtua Fighter forum, Hit Flashes, Sparks, Effects, Auras](https://host.virtuafighter.com/threads/hit-flashes-sparks-effects-auras-whats-the-difference.21696)
- **[DEV, via SECONDARY]** Riot: the size and intensity of an effect must match the importance of the attack; a basic attack should not look like an ultimate. — [VFX Apprentice, 10 Design Tips](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips)

**Colour and value**
- **[DEV, via SECONDARY]** Riot: avoid absolute 0% black and 100% white; build the body of effects in mid values; reserve the highest value spikes for the instantaneous primary flash (impact point, explosion core). VFX get a higher saturation ceiling than environment (muted) and UI (controlled) so they cut through the background. Use one dominant colour with a desaturated accent limited to small highlights. A second summary of the same guide says 100% white is reserved for ultimates; the two summaries differ on whether pure white is ever allowed. — [VFX Apprentice, 10 Design Tips](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips); the differing summary: [VFX Apprentice, Creating VFX style guides](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/creating-vfx-style-guides-for-games)
- **[DEV feedback]** A praised hit spark ran its hues from white through yellow and orange to red; the suggested fix for a noisy outer ring was to hold it in a narrow orange-red range. — [realtimevfx.com, Stylised Hit-spark thread](https://realtimevfx.com/t/stylised-explosive-and-fiery-hit-spark-looking-for-feedback/11286)
- **[BOOK, via notes]** "Smoke is 1/3 shadow, 2/3 light." — [Student notes on Elemental Magic Vol. I](https://amystevenshonours.wordpress.com/2014/10/06/elemental-magic/)
- **[DEV: Capcom, via SECONDARY]** SF6 fixed blue as "effectively the colour of blocking", and reused it for parry. — [EventHubs report on Capcom's SF6 developer column](https://eventhubs.com/news/2023/jul/18/capcom-early-sf6-drive-parry)

### Inferences
- A shape vocabulary by attack type can be derived from the cited rules (direction follows force, sharper means more dangerous, block is soft and stops in front): blunt hits as a round burst centred on the contact point; slashes as a thin tapered streak along the blade path; pierces as a narrow cone or line along the thrust; energy hits with a ring and glow. No source I opened states this mapping in these words; it is my synthesis.
- Hit versus block should differ in silhouette, colour and length at once (SF6: O versus X, warm versus blue; community implementation: 16 versus 8 frames), so the result is readable even in a single frame.
- A size ladder of about +17% per strength level (1.0, 1.17, 1.33, with 1.5 and 1.67 for special and super) is the only numeric ladder found. It is a community author's taste, not an industry standard.
- The white-hot core, saturated middle, darker edge rule requested in the brief is supported by the Riot value rule (highest values only at the impact point) and by the white-to-red hue ramp praised in the critique; I found no source stating it as a three-band rule.

### Gaps
- No source gave spark size relative to character height (for example, as a percentage of the body). Sizes are only given as relative scales.
- No professional source I could open gives a written slash versus blunt versus pierce shape guide for fighting games.
- Riot's original wording on value (whether 100% white is banned or reserved) could not be checked; the two summaries conflict.
- No source specified tapering or pointed-end rules for streaks in numbers (length-to-width ratios and so on).

## 4. Fighting-game specifics: hit versus block, strengths, counter hits, per-character and per-element effects

### Takeaway
Across the games for which I found statements, the feedback system has the same parts: a spark whose size tracks strength, a distinct block effect (softer, bluer, shorter, stopping in front of the defender), longer hitstop for stronger hits, and extra screen-level treatment for counter hits (added hitstop for the victim, slowdown, camera zoom, a flash). Developer statements with real detail exist for Street Fighter 6, Smash Bros. and Skullgirls; for Guilty Gear, KOF and the rest I only have community frame data and visual descriptions.

### Cited Findings

**Street Fighter**
- **[DEV: Capcom, via SECONDARY]** SF6's Drive System effects were all based on the Drive Impact effect. The first Drive Parry prototype showed only sweat and smoke flying between the fighters; it was reworked into the paint-splash look. Regular and Just parries get a paint splash; Just (Perfect) parries add a larger silhouette behind the character and ink splashes. Blue is the block colour. The parry light forms an X against the hit effect's O. — [EventHubs report on Capcom's SF6 developer column "effects003"](https://eventhubs.com/news/2023/jul/18/capcom-early-sf6-drive-parry)
- **[DEV: Capcom]** SF6 effects are built from many staged assets per move (Hadoken: buildup, launch, flight, impact) and artists can set effect start times and delays themselves, tied to the character animation timeline. — [Capcom R&D RE:2023 slides](https://www.docswell.com/s/CAPCOM_RandD/538NVE-RE2023)
- **[COMMUNITY DATA, snippet only]** SF6: heavier attacks have longer hitstop; some Punish Counters have extra long hitstop. — [SuperCombo wiki mirror, SF6 Game Data](https://srk.shib.live/w/Street_Fighter_6/Game_Data)
- **[FAN expert]** Older Street Fighter: SF2 series about 14 frames of freeze at every strength; most later games about 9 / 11 / 13 for light / medium / heavy; SFIV examples of 8-12 frames. Hit and block freeze are generally equal. — [Sonic Hurricane, Impact Freeze](https://sonichurricane.com/?p=1043)
- **[Unreliable, do not use as fact]** One web page lists SF6 "light hit-stop of 8-10 frames" and particle counts of 15-30 for light hits with no source. — [mygamedesign page](https://mygamedesign-frontend.pages.dev/how-do-you-make-melee-combat-feel-crunchy-and-satisfying)

**Guilty Gear, BlazBlue, Dragon Ball FighterZ (Arc System Works)**
- **[COMMUNITY DATA, snippet only]** Guilty Gear Xrd: hitstop, hitstun and blockstun all scale with an Attack Level from 0 to 4; hitstop is 11 / 12 / 13 / 14 / 15 frames. Individual moves can override the defaults. — [Dustloop, GGXRD Attack Attributes](https://dustloop.com/w/GGXRD/Attack_Attributes); [Dustloop, Elphelt frame data](https://www.dustloop.com/w/GGXRD-R2/Elphelt_Valentine/Frame_Data)
- **[COMMUNITY DATA, snippet only]** Guilty Gear Strive: a counter hit adds 10% damage and, depending on its type, extra hitstop or slowdown. "The larger the Counter Hit type, the more dramatic the visuals and audio": louder audio cues, longer hitstop, longer slowdown, and for Large counter hits a camera zoom-in. In Strive the counter-hit hitstop applies to both characters; in Xrd only to the receiver. — [Dustloop, GGST Attack Attributes (cached page)](https://www.dustloop.com/wiki/images/cache/f/fc/GGST%252FAttack_Attributes.html); [Dustloop, GGXRD Attack Attributes](https://dustloop.com/w/GGXRD/Attack_Attributes)
- **[DEV: Arc System Works]** Xrd's look comes from "limited animation": hand-keyed poses with no automatic in-betweens, chosen for clarity. The GDC talk covers character shading and animation, not VFX. — [GDC Vault, GuiltyGearXrd's Art Style: The X Factor Between 2D and 3D](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022031/GuiltyGearXrd-s-Art-Style-The)
- **[FAN observation by a known technical artist, no credentials shown on the page]** Dragon Ball FighterZ effects: "They do a lot of traditional keyframed 2d style animation, but as multiple individual 3d meshes." Other posters in the thread note that Arc System Works has published nothing detailed on its effects process. — [realtimevfx.com, Dragonball Fighter Z thread](https://realtimevfx.com/t/dragonball-fighter-z/2522)

**King of Fighters**
- **[COMMUNITY DATA, snippet only]** KOF XIII: light attacks 7 frames of hitstop, heavy attacks 11. — [SuperCombo wiki mirror, KOF XIII](https://srk.shib.live/w/The_King_of_Fighters_XIII/Character_States/Hitstun_Blockstun_state)

**Skullgirls**
- **[SECONDARY]** The hit spark carries system information: when the Infinite Prevention System detects a loop, the hit sparks change colour and the victim can burst out. Sources disagree on the colour: Giant Bomb says red; a fan review says from light yellow to "a very noticeable pinkish purple"; a later patch note says sparks "no longer change to pink" when comboing an assist. — [Giant Bomb, Skullgirls](https://www.giantbomb.com/skullgirls/3030-34082/); [Dual Pixels review](https://dualpixels.com/?p=511); [Dustloop forums patch notes](https://www.forums.dustloop.com/index.html/home/incoming-patch-shift-r251/)
- **[DEV patch notes]** Skullgirls Encore added a "Counter Hit Flash" when hitting an opponent out of an attack. — [Skullgirls Encore patch notes](https://skullgirls.com/?p=1054)
- **[SECONDARY]** Skullgirls Encore introduced palettized hit effects. — [The Fighters Generation, Skullgirls Encore](https://www.fightersgeneration.com/games/skullgirls-encore.html)
- **[DEV: Mariel Cartwright]** Animation side: punches may have only six frames; one anticipation frame, an overshoot frame and smears carry the power. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020575/Animation-Bootcamp-Fluid-and-Powerful); [80.lv summary](https://80.lv/articles/tips-on-making-fluid-and-powerful-animations)

**Smash Bros. (per-element and per-hitbox variation, developer-stated)**
- **[DEV: Sakurai]** Hitstop depends on more than damage: each hitbox has its own modifier (the tip of Marth's sword has more, the blade edge less); electric attacks get more automatically; projectiles get less. The victim shakes during the freeze with decaying amplitude, scaled by camera distance. — [Source Gaming translation of Sakurai's column](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/)
- **[COMMUNITY DATA]** Electric multiplier 1.5x; hitlag caps of 20 frames (Melee) and 30 frames (Brawl onward). — [SmashWiki, Hitlag](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Hitlag)

**General conventions**
- **[FAN, snippet only]** Spark size encodes damage; a separate effect encodes hit type. Mixing the two makes the feedback harder to read. — [Virtua Fighter forum thread](https://host.virtuafighter.com/threads/hit-flashes-sparks-effects-auras-whats-the-difference.21696)
- **[FAN / community implementation]** Specials and supers add a layer behind the defender and a brighter impact layer (impact alpha 192 for specials and 256 for supers, against 128 for normals), on top of the size increase. — [Mugen Fighters Guild, P.o.t.S creation diary part 6](https://mugenguild.com/forum/topics/diary-creation-p-o-t-s-character-part-6-santas-little-helpers-191095.0.html)
- **[FAN / community implementation]** Sparks are spawned at a position set relative to the defender; in MUGEN a negative offset puts the spark deeper inside the defender. — [Mugen Fighters Guild forum message](https://mugenguild.com/forum/msg.2234891)

### Inferences
- A counter hit is communicated mostly outside the spark itself: extra freeze on the victim, slowdown, zoom, a flash, a louder sound. A counter-hit variant of the spark can therefore be the same spark as the equivalent normal hit plus a screen-level layer, which keeps the family consistent.
- The spark is a legitimate channel for system state (Skullgirls' colour change), provided the default colours are held constant enough for the change to stand out.
- Low hits are not treated as a separate category by any source I found; games classify by strength, outcome (hit, block, parry, counter) and element, with position following the contact point.

### Gaps
- No developer statement or frame-by-frame analysis with frame counts was found for the hit sparks of Street Fighter III, IV or V, BlazBlue, KOF, Marvel vs. Capcom or Dragon Ball FighterZ. Art books and CEDEC sessions were not reachable; the only SF6 CEDEC 2024 VFX session found is about physics simulation, not hit effects ([CGWORLD report](https://cgworld.jp/article/202410-cedec03-sf6.html)).
- Capcom's original SF6 developer column on effects (streetfighter.com, "effects003" and presumably 001 and 002) was not located; only EventHubs' translation was read.
- No source described how low or crouching hits, launchers or sweeps are differentiated visually.
- The exact Skullgirls spark colours and its per-strength hitstop defaults remain unconfirmed.
- Nothing citable was found on per-character hit sparks in the named games (for instance, whether any of them changes the generic spark per character, as opposed to per element or weapon).

## 5. 2D FX animation craft: principles from professional sources

### Takeaway
The craft rule that recurs in every professional source is to animate the energy, not the object: design the force and its path first, draw fast and rough, let the effect burst and then hand over to outside forces (gravity, air) as it dies, and keep shapes irregular, asymmetrical and readable in silhouette. Anime action animation adds the split-second impact frame and stylised debris as its signature tools.

### Cited Findings

**Gilland (Elemental Magic)**
- "Effects are all about energy and the underlying forms that make up this energy." Focus on "the forces underlying the effects, the patterns in motion, the timing and the physics". Plan with grids and arrows showing the energy flow before drawing. — [Student notes on Elemental Magic Vol. I](https://amystevenshonours.wordpress.com/2014/10/06/elemental-magic/)
- Reviewers summarise the book in one word, energy: "Animating special effects is essentially animating energy." It is a "how to approach" book, not a "how to draw" book. Its chapters go from design, scale and perspective to energy, action path and timing. — [Parka Blogs review](https://www.parkablogs.com/content/book-review-elemental-magic-art-of-special-effects-animation); [animationbooks.tv review](https://animationbooks.tv/2016/03/20/elemental-magic-volume-i/)
- "Many different special effects events start out looking like an explosion, but soon afterwards the materials involved return to their natural state." A splash "starts out looking like an explosion with a great deal of directional energy" and "becomes more fluid and 'water-shaped' as it loses its energy". "The first frame of an explosion looks exactly the same, regardless of the element that is involved." — [Gilland, Elemental Magic Vol. II, pp. 97, 248 (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt)
- Breakup: decay "begins as the splash is still shooting upward and continues through until the last droplet"; holes "increase in size and number very quickly, within a mere 24 frames or one second"; they must not be repetitive or evenly spaced. — [Gilland, Elemental Magic Vol. II, pp. 98-100 (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt)
- After an explosion "the smoke will begin to take on the billowing, mushrooming bubblelike shapes" of the air currents; spent smoke and debris are "more and more affected by other outside forces, like gravity, air pressure, wind". Dust settles under gravity "before long"; steam vanishes "very quickly"; smoke can linger. — [Gilland, Elemental Magic Vol. II, pp. 256, 259 (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt)
- Smoke "has to come from the force that generated it, as well as from its own forces"; its lifespan depends on its density. Informing energy (small air movements) is distinct from resolving energy (gravity pulling particles down). — [Student notes on Elemental Magic Vol. I](https://amystevenshonours.wordpress.com/2014/10/06/elemental-magic/)
- Solid impacts: a breaking mug sends "broken shards shooting outward at an extremely high speed". — [Gilland, Elemental Magic Vol. II, p. 245 (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt)
- Sparks and pixie dust: each particle has a lifespan; "many particles can be allowed to appear randomly out of nowhere and then disappear"; "a really elegant pixie dust design does not necessarily need to have millions of densely packed particles"; it is "usually animated straight ahead", starting from very rough drawings. — [Gilland, Elemental Magic Vol. II, pp. 289-304 (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt)
- Scale is sold by timing: large effects need "large drawings" moved "much more slowly"; "the timing is crucial if we want the splash to have the correct scale". — [Gilland, Elemental Magic Vol. II, pp. 111, 121 (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt)
- Gilland discourages "canned" digital effects for explosions. — [Elemental Magic Vol. II text, quoted via search](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt)

**Anime action animation (sakuga)**
- Impact frames: "special frames drawn by animators that appear for a split second, often added to make an impact more pronounced". The earliest online description is dated to a Sakugabooru post of August 2013. — [Sakugabooru wiki](https://www.sakugabooru.com/wiki/show?title=impact_frames); [Know Your Meme, Impact Frames](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/cultures/impact-frames)
- Yutaka Nakamura is credited as one of the first mainstream animators to use impact frames (Cowboy Bebop is named), and is known for cube-shaped debris nicknamed "yutapon cubes", camera-shake blurs, and bending and stretching of fast objects. — [Wikipedia-derived article on Yutaka Nakamura](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Yutaka_Nakamura); [AniList staff page](https://anilist.co/staff/101801/Yutaka-Nakamura); [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/cultures/impact-frames)
- A cut-by-cut fan analysis of Nakamura's My Hero Academia episode 68 work describes his effects as "densely layered". I could not open the page (server error), so only the search summary is available. — [Artist Unknown, cut-by-cut analysis](https://artistunknown.info/?p=612)
- Caution: "Nakamura debris" in some writing refers to Takashi Nakamura, a different animator. — [Animétudes, Takashi Nakamura spotlight](https://animetudes.com/2021/05/30/takashi-nakamura-spotlight/)
- An animation course outline lists impact frames with "the risk of impact frames", suggesting overuse is a recognised pitfall. — [Coloso 2D animator course page](https://coloso.global/products/2danimator-proro-us)

**Game VFX professionals**
- Stretch and smear frames are taught as the way to make quick action effects stand out. — [VFX Apprentice, FX Timing Principles course](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/courses/fx-timing-principles)
- Timing patterns: "impulse" (instant bursts or crescendos) versus "rhythm" (looping or varying). Faster than a heartbeat reads as aggressive; healing effects should pace under 60 BPM and attacks the opposite. More parts allow more sophisticated timing "but also more risk of dissonance". — [VFX Apprentice, The Soul of Effects](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/the-soul-of-effects-what-is-timing-in-vfx)
- "Having a natural dissipation, especially on big gameplay moments, provides clarity and satisfaction" (League VFX developer, quoted via search summary). — [VFX Apprentice, Creating VFX style guides](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/creating-vfx-style-guides-for-games)
- Lingering particles cost overdraw; even a few extra frames of dissipation matter on weak machines. — [VFX Apprentice, 10 Design Tips](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips)

### Inferences
- Gilland's "first frame of every explosion looks the same" supports sharing one flash drawing across the whole family of hit effects and letting only the following frames express the character or element.
- His "starts as an explosion, then the material returns to its natural state" gives a recipe for per-element variation: identical burst for the first frames, then fire licks upward, smoke billows, water falls, sparks arc under gravity.
- The dissipation should follow the forces (debris falls, smoke rises and curls) with irregular holes, instead of fading the whole drawing uniformly; a uniform fade is what the Riot critique called an abrupt clip.
- A full-screen impact frame is a rare, high-cost emphasis in anime; by analogy it belongs on counters, supers and finishing blows only.

### Gaps
- Nothing by Michel Gagné or Adam Phillips was retrieved; no primary material on Skullgirls' FX pipeline was found (only the character-animation talk and the note about palettized hit effects).
- No Sakuga Blog article on effects animation or impact frames was reachable; the impact-frame details asked for (monochrome or inverted colours, exactly 1-2 frames) are not confirmed by any source I could open. The closest primary support is Gilland's "negative frame followed by a black-white frame".
- Gilland Vol. I was only available through reviews and a student's notes; Vol. II's clean-up and frame-rate chapters were not in the portions read.
- No slash-specific craft source (design and timing of sword arcs) was found in this pass.

## 6. Keeping a family of effects consistent across a roster while giving each character an identity

### Takeaway
The documented method is a shared skeleton with controlled variables: the same layers, timing and value hierarchy for every character, with identity carried by palette, shape motif and one or two added layers, and with certain colours and silhouettes reserved for system meanings such as block. Skullgirls' palettized hit effects and Street Fighter 6's rule that all Drive effects derive from one base effect are the two concrete precedents found.

### Cited Findings
- **[DEV: Capcom, via SECONDARY]** SF6 built every Drive System effect from one base ("Our basis for the Drive System effects was the Drive Impact"), fixed one colour per meaning (blue for block and parry), and separated outcomes by silhouette (O for hit, X for parry). — [EventHubs report on Capcom's SF6 developer column](https://eventhubs.com/news/2023/jul/18/capcom-early-sf6-drive-parry)
- **[SECONDARY]** Skullgirls Encore made its hit effects palettized, so one set of drawings can be recoloured. — [The Fighters Generation, Skullgirls Encore](https://www.fightersgeneration.com/games/skullgirls-encore.html)
- **[SECONDARY]** Skullgirls reserves a spark colour change for a system state (infinite detected), which only works because the normal spark colour is consistent. — [Giant Bomb, Skullgirls](https://www.giantbomb.com/skullgirls/3030-34082/)
- **[FAN / community implementation]** One spark design serves five strengths through scale alone (0.30 to 0.50), with one added back layer and a brighter impact layer for specials and supers; some authors recolour for specials and supers, "classic" authors only resize. All use the same 16-tick lifetime. — [Mugen Fighters Guild, P.o.t.S creation diary part 6](https://mugenguild.com/forum/topics/diary-creation-p-o-t-s-character-part-6-santas-little-helpers-191095.0.html)
- **[DEV: Riot VFX artist]** "For every unique ability in the game, you should associate some unique design language." Designers rate each spell's importance from 1 to 10 in a visual-hierarchy document and the VFX must match that rating. — [realtimevfx.com, Anatomy of a League of Legends missile, part 2](https://realtimevfx.com/t/anatomy-of-a-league-of-legends-missile-part-2-what-are-we-communicating-with-our-vfx/13062)
- **[DEV, via SECONDARY]** Riot's guide is organised into five areas that every effect must satisfy: gameplay, value, colour, shape, timing. It prescribes one dominant colour per effect with a restrained accent, and shared colour conventions (healing leans green, frost blue, gunpowder orange-red). — [VFX Apprentice, Creating VFX style guides](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/creating-vfx-style-guides-for-games); [VFX Apprentice, 10 Design Tips](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips)
- **[DEV: Sakurai]** Variation by element and by hitbox is applied as modifiers on a common rule (electric gets more hitstop automatically; sword tip more than blade), not as separate systems. — [Source Gaming translation of Sakurai's column](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/)
- **[BOOK]** The first frame of an explosion is the same for every element; the material's own character appears afterwards. — [Gilland, Elemental Magic Vol. II, p. 248 (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/stream/elemental-magic-volume-ii/Elemental_Magic_2C_Volume_II_djvu.txt)
- **[DEV]** In fighting games the effect must match the damage exactly to protect "competitive integrity". — [VFX Apprentice, The Soul of Effects](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/the-soul-of-effects-what-is-timing-in-vfx)

### Inferences
- A specification that follows from the findings: hold constant across the roster the layer order, the total duration per outcome (for example 16 frames for hit, 8 for block), the flash drawing, the size ladder per strength and the value hierarchy; vary per character only the palette (through palettized sprites), the shape motif of the burst and streaks after the first frames, and one optional signature layer.
- Reserve a small set of colours and silhouettes for system meanings that no character palette may use (block colour, counter-hit flash, any warning state), following SF6 and Skullgirls.
- Write the family down as a table like Riot's importance scale: one row per outcome (light, medium, heavy, low, blocked, counter), with size, layers, duration, hitstop and screen-level extras, so new characters fill in only the identity columns.
- Variation per element is best expressed as modifiers on the shared rule (Sakurai), for example an electric character adding hitstop and a jitter layer, instead of a wholly separate effect.

### Gaps
- No studio's actual internal style guide for fighting-game hit effects was found; the Riot guide is for a top-down MOBA and its numeric rules were only available second-hand.
- No source gives rules for how far a per-character spark may depart from the generic one before readability suffers, nor examples of per-character hit sparks in the named fighting games with developer commentary.
- How palettized hit effects are authored in Skullgirls (number of palette entries, how the colours are assigned per character or per move) was not documented in anything I could open.
