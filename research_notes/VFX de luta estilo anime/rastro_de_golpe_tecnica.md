# Melee swing trails / slash trails ("air cut") in stylized and anime-style action and fighting games: real-time VFX technique

Reading notes for the report writer:

- Source quality is marked on each finding: **[pro]** = working VFX artist or studio, or official engine documentation; **[tutorial]** = independent tutorial author (not a studio breakdown); **[hobby]** = hobbyist or beginner source; **[secondary]** = a summary of a primary source that I could not open myself.
- Several primary sources could not be opened (Riot VFX style guide PDF, Guilty Gear Xrd GDC handout PDF, UDK AnimTrails page, Polycount thread, Niagara ribbon reference page). Where a fact comes only from a search-result snippet or a third-party summary of those, it is marked **[snippet]** or **[secondary]**. Treat those as leads, not as verified quotes.
- Formulas are written as shader-like pseudocode. `u` runs along the trail (0 = one end, 1 = other end), `v` runs across the width, unless stated otherwise.

## 1. Geometry: how is the trail shape produced?

### Takeaway
Professionals use two main constructions: (a) a pre-modelled arc/ring/disc mesh with straightened ("flat") UVs on which a clamped streak texture is scrolled once, so the mesh never moves and the texture does the swing; and (b) a runtime ribbon built from two sockets on the weapon, where every cross-section has its own age. Hand-drawn 2D image sequences and per-frame modelled meshes are what the one anime-style fighting game with a public breakdown (Guilty Gear Xrd) uses for hits and smoke; I found no public breakdown of how its sword trails are built.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Pre-modelled arc mesh ("slash mesh", "soulercoaster")**
- [pro] A slash is "a mesh in the shape of an arc, that is revealed in sync with the sword swing"; the alternative is a ribbon that follows the sword tip. For straight cuts: "stretched sprites or mesh planes" (poster AW_VFX). — [realtimevfx: General question about sword slash vfx](https://realtimevfx.com/t/general-question-about-sword-slash-vfx/26396)
- [pro] A "soulercoaster" is "a trail that flows along custom built mesh with flat uvs" and is recommended for weapon slashes. The mesh starts as a stretched plane whose UVs are straight and fill 0-1 with the texture's scroll axis along the length; UV layout steps: unitize, stitch edges, lay out along U, snap one side of verts to the UV edge (posters Augie, Chaybi). — [realtimevfx: VFX Basics - Mesh Soulercoasters](https://realtimevfx.com/t/vfx-basics-mesh-soulercoasters/1987)
- [pro] To be visible from more angles, curve the mesh cross-section to about one third of a cylinder and use a double-sided material (poster Travis). — [realtimevfx: VFX Basics - Mesh Soulercoasters](https://realtimevfx.com/t/vfx-basics-mesh-soulercoasters/1987)
- [pro] A flat plane seen edge-on disappears ("the player will see nothing"); fix suggested is a sprite/plane with a locked axis that still faces the camera, and running the particle system in local space so rotating the system rotates the slash (posters Jyhrie, Travis). — [realtimevfx: General question about sword slash vfx](https://realtimevfx.com/t/general-question-about-sword-slash-vfx/26396)
- [tutorial] Cyanilux's slash uses a ring mesh surrounding the player with its origin at the ring centre; the mesh is static and the shader pans a mask across it. It is "similar to that of a soulercoaster". He notes the ring could be modelled more slash-shaped rather than a perfect circle, and that the alternative to a mesh renderer is a particle system emitting one mesh particle. — [Cyanilux: Sword Slash Shader Breakdown](https://www.cyanilux.com/tutorials/sword-slash-shader-breakdown/)
- [pro] "The core of the effect is a ring shaped mesh with an alpha mask that covers the center of the sword slash"; "The Noise 1 texture is scrolled across the mesh and drives the slash movement (the mesh is static)" (poster Geri, built with a commercial Unity toolkit). — [realtimevfx: Venom Slash breakdown](https://realtimevfx.com/t/venom-slash-breakdown-of-the-effect-included/18903)
- [pro] For anime-style attacks specifically: the slash is "a slash texture scrolled across a flat disc mesh, with alpha erosion fading it out"; scroll and erosion amounts are exposed and driven by curves over the particle lifetime (poster Qriva). — [Unity Discussions: How do I make vfx for attacks in Anime style?](https://discussions.unity.com/t/how-do-i-make-vfx-for-attacks-in-anime-style/879490)
- [snippet] In another breakdown the mesh UVs were "scrunched and widened near the edges to speed up and slow down the panning", i.e. UV density is used as a built-in speed curve. I only saw this in a search snippet attributed to a realtimevfx breakdown thread. — [realtimevfx: Luke Mason WoW student art contest entry](https://realtimevfx.com/t/luke-mason-wow-student-art-contest-2019-entry/7322/19)

**(b) Runtime ribbons that follow a bone or socket**
- [pro] Ribbons are "a series of connected sprites", where each slice acts as an individual particle with its own lifetime (poster Travis). — [realtimevfx: Shannon Berke LoL FX thread, page 7](https://realtimevfx.com/t/shannon-berke-lol-fx-knowledge-share/1133?page=7)
- [pro] Riot artist Shannon Berke: with a ribbon or trail emitter the erosion plays "over each quads lifetime"; "If you use one mesh, the whole texture will erode making it look uniform". — [realtimevfx: Shannon Berke LoL FX thread, page 7](https://realtimevfx.com/t/shannon-berke-lol-fx-knowledge-share/1133?page=7)
- [pro] Unreal `AnimNotifyState_Trail` (official API): the trail is defined by `first_socket_name` and `second_socket_name`; `width_scale_mode` chooses where width is scaled from (Centre = from the midpoint between the two sockets, First = from the first socket, Second = from the second socket; scale 1.0 leaves width unchanged, 0.0 gives zero width); `width_scale_curve` names an animation curve that drives the width; `ps_template` is the particle system used. — [Unreal Python API: AnimNotifyState_Trail](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/python-api/class/AnimNotifyState_Trail)
- [pro] Anim trails are described as "A fairly less painful way to do sword slashes" (poster veer); a user placed "the two sockets at the start and end of the sword" and planned to pull the end socket further out to enlarge the trail. — [realtimevfx: Sword Slash help](https://realtimevfx.com/t/sword-slash-help/9407)
- [snippet] Unreal forum description of how the engine builds an anim trail: it samples the positions of the two sockets each tick and stitches triangles between them along the trail's length. Seen only in a search snippet. — [Unreal forums: How do I hook up Anim Trails?](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/how-do-i-hook-up-anim-trails/285533)
- [pro] Unity Trail Renderer (official manual): `Time` = "the lifetime of a point in the trail, in seconds"; `Min Vertex Distance` = "The minimum distance between points in the trail, in world units"; `Width` is a value times a curve along the length, "sampled at each vertex, so its accuracy is limited by the number of vertices"; `Color` is a gradient along the length, sampled per vertex and linearly interpolated; `Emitting` pauses/unpauses point generation; `Alignment` is View (faces camera) or TransformZ. Texture modes: Stretch ("Map the texture once along the entire length of the trail"), Tile (repeat by world-unit length), DistributePerSegment (once along the length assuming evenly spaced vertices), RepeatPerSegment (once per segment). — [Unity Manual: Trail Renderer](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/current/manual/visual-effects/lines-trails-billboards/rendering-trails/class-trail-renderer)
- [snippet] Niagara ribbon UV distribution modes in the official API include "Scaled Uniformly" (UVs spread over the ribbon without repeating) and "Tiled From Start Over Ribbon Length" (tiling by distance along the ribbon times a `TilingLength`, "traintrack" style); separate leading-edge and trailing-edge modes control how UVs transition in and out of life. From search snippets of the official enum pages; the reference page itself returned empty. — [Unreal API: ENiagaraRibbonUVDistributionMode](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/Niagara/ENiagaraRibbonUVDistributionMode)
- [pro] Known ribbon weakness: UE4 ribbons show artifacts and break-up and "aren't smooth as in games like Battlerite, LoL" (poster OgnjenIlkic, no fix given). — [realtimevfx: How to do this trail? Battlerite](https://realtimevfx.com/t/how-to-do-this-trail-battlerite/2943?page=2)
- [pro] Battlerite-style trail recreation: "I used a rotating source and scaled the trails width over its lifetime"; the texture "just has those strokes painted in, and you scale the ribbon/trail over time" (posters Limeslushie, NateLane). — [realtimevfx: How to do this trail? Battlerite](https://realtimevfx.com/t/how-to-do-this-trail-battlerite/2943?page=2)

**(c) Hand-drawn flipbooks / image sequences**
- [secondary] Guilty Gear Xrd (GDC 2015 talk by Junya C. Motomura, via an AI-generated video summary): hit effects use a sequence of 2D images "switching every frame"; jump smoke is made by "a brute-force method where each frame is modeled separately"; the same summary says most effects are particle-based. — [videohighlight summary of the GDC talk](https://videohighlight.com/v/yhGjCzxJV3E)

**(d) Smear frames in the character animation**
- [pro] Overwatch senior animator David Gibson (GDC talk, reported by Kotaku): "I like to think of them as making up for a lack of motion blur. So when something moves really fast, I want to help you try and see it"; "We try to put smear frames in whenever we can"; on one pose, "I just yanked his arm way out". Smears are mostly used where the camera is fixed because they look odd from other angles. — [Kotaku: This Is Not An Overwatch Glitch](https://kotaku.com/this-is-not-an-overwatch-glitch-1777612546)
- [secondary] Guilty Gear Xrd: each frame is treated as a keyframe "akin to stop-motion animation"; characters have about 500 bones; scale animation was done manually to allow exaggerated movement; mesh deformation is applied at every keyframe. — [videohighlight summary of the GDC talk](https://videohighlight.com/v/yhGjCzxJV3E)
- [snippet] The Xrd GDC handout states the team stopped using interpolation between key frames: every frame is a key frame posed by the animator. Search snippet of the handout PDF, which I could not download. — [GDC handout PDF (Arc System Works)](https://www.ggxrd.com/Motomura_Junya_GuiltyGearXrd.pdf)

### Inferences
- The arc mesh and the ribbon are the same idea with the "time" axis stored in different places. On an arc mesh, `u` along the arc is the swing's timeline and one material parameter (a reveal offset) plays it. On a ribbon, each cross-section stores its own age. A custom engine can get the ribbon's main benefit (erosion that starts at the tail and travels forward) on a static arc mesh by computing a per-pixel age from `u` and the reveal parameter (see section 3 inferences).
- When to use which, as far as the sources support it: a pre-modelled arc is preferred when the attack animation is fixed and authored (fighting-game moves, anime-style attacks: Qriva, AW_VFX, Cyanilux, Geri all use it), because shape and timing are fully art-directed and independent of frame rate or bone sampling. Ribbons are preferred when the motion is not known in advance or must follow the weapon exactly (veer, Unreal anim trails), at the cost of the smoothness problems reported for UE4 ribbons.
- For a 2D game the "arc mesh with straightened UVs" is a triangle strip between an inner and outer radius over an angle range, with `u` = angle fraction and `v` = radius fraction. The edge-on visibility problem (Jyhrie/Travis) does not exist in 2D.
- Guilty Gear Xrd's public material describes hits (2D image sequence) and smoke (one mesh per frame), which suggests the studio favours fully authored, frame-by-frame effects over simulated ones. That its weapon trails are also authored meshes is a reasonable guess but is not stated in anything I could open.

## 2. Texture: what does a slash/trail texture look like and what is in each channel?

### Takeaway
The standard set is small: one clamped shape texture (painted streaks or a triangle/wedge that is bright at the leading end and tapers to a point), one tiling noise for break-up and erosion, and optionally a gradient for colour. Artists pack these as separate grayscale maps in R, G, B, and the one explicit trail breakdown found uses R+G for distortion and end-dissolve noise and B for the main shape.

### Cited Findings
- [pro] Channel packing in a trail breakdown (Ryan Zeng, Unity + Amplify, texture made in Substance Designer): "RG: Work for distort the uv and the end of the trail dissolved. B: The main shape of trail." — [realtimevfx: RzFX Flow trail breakdown](https://realtimevfx.com/t/rzfx-flow-trail-breakdown/6483)
- [pro] Riot's Shannon Berke: the trail texture and the erosion texture are "two different textures". — [realtimevfx: Shannon Berke LoL FX thread, page 7](https://realtimevfx.com/t/shannon-berke-lol-fx-knowledge-share/1133?page=7)
- [pro] Painted-stroke approach: "your texture just has those strokes painted in, and you scale the ribbon/trail over time" (NateLane). — [realtimevfx: How to do this trail? Battlerite](https://realtimevfx.com/t/how-to-do-this-trail-battlerite/2943?page=2)
- [pro] How streak textures were painted for a slash sketch (Erik Forsström, "Axelorio"): lines of varying thickness painted in Photoshop, duplicated, given motion blur and Gaussian blur, blended, plus the smudge tool; only two textures were used and he notes one would have been enough. — [realtimevfx: Erik Forsström Sketch #14](https://realtimevfx.com/t/erik-forsstrom-sketch-14-wip/5167)
- [pro] V-shaped (double streak) trail: the texture is "split into 2 bands of noise, with most of the whiter values in the center of each band" (Kris_Wilkins). — [realtimevfx: Shannon Berke LoL FX thread, page 7](https://realtimevfx.com/t/shannon-berke-lol-fx-knowledge-share/1133?page=7)
- [tutorial] Cyanilux uses a triangle-shaped mask texture (`SlashTex`) imported with Clamp wrap mode, plus a seamless noise texture; the triangle gives the pointed tail. — [Cyanilux: Sword Slash Shader Breakdown](https://www.cyanilux.com/tutorials/sword-slash-shader-breakdown/)
- [pro] Shape textures that scroll once must be clamped, not wrapped: "what we need is the texture to be clamped on the x and y axis"; with wrap the trail tiles back onto the mesh after it finishes (Augie). — [realtimevfx: VFX Basics - Mesh Soulercoasters](https://realtimevfx.com/t/vfx-basics-mesh-soulercoasters/1987)
- [pro] Erosion and mask textures hold a numeric progression, not colour, so they must be imported linear (sRGB off), otherwise the erosion progresses non-linearly. — [vfxdoc: Alpha erosion](https://vfxdoc.readthedocs.io/en/latest/shaders/alpha-erosion/)
- [pro] Equalize the noise histogram (Photoshop: Image > Adjustments > Equalize) so that an erosion value of 0.1 reveals about 10% of texels, giving a linear, predictable reveal (MikeH). — [realtimevfx: Alpha/Opacity Mask/Clip/Erode workflow](https://realtimevfx.com/t/what-is-your-alpha-opacity-mask-clip-erode-animation-workflow-like/7705)
- [pro] When authoring dissolve ramps, avoid pure white and pure black to leave headroom for shader-side adjustment (yunus). — [realtimevfx: Alpha/Opacity Mask/Clip/Erode workflow](https://realtimevfx.com/t/what-is-your-alpha-opacity-mask-clip-erode-animation-workflow-like/7705)
- [pro] Precision limit when packing gradients in compressed textures: R and B channels have 5 bits (32 gray levels) and G has 6 bits (64 levels), so put the gradient that needs the smoothest erosion in G (Simon Trümpler, "simonschreibt"). — [realtimevfx: Alpha/Opacity Mask/Clip/Erode workflow](https://realtimevfx.com/t/what-is-your-alpha-opacity-mask-clip-erode-animation-workflow-like/7705)
- [pro] Diablo 3 convention (Julian Love's GDC 2013 talk as relayed by Nick Seavert of JangaFX): noise in RGB, shape in alpha; "The more you multiply, the more you want to constrain the range of your noise"; "Once you have a lot of black in your texture, it eats up a lot of action". — [JangaFX: Exploring and Modernizing The VFX Methods of Diablo 3](https://jangafx.com/insights/diablo-3-vfx-experiments)
- [secondary] Riot style guide as summarized by VFX Apprentice: avoid photographic textures and noisy procedural detail; shapes should be hand-drawn and painted, mixing soft and hard forms. — [VFX Apprentice: 10 LoL VFX design tips](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips)
- [hobby] Packed textures store separate grayscale maps in R, G and B; a grayscale map goes to opacity; masked (cutout) mode only gives 0 or 1. — [DMmotionarts: Mayu's Guide to VFX](https://dmmotionarts.com/mayus-guide-to-vfx-fundamentals-and-techniques/)

### Inferences
- Irregular, pointed streak ends come from three sources that combine: (1) the shape texture itself tapers (triangle/wedge, or painted strokes of different lengths that each end in a point); (2) a noise multiplied or subtracted near the tail eats the streaks unevenly, so each streak ends at a different place; (3) the width curve of the ribbon/mesh narrows towards the tail. No source states this as one rule; it is the common denominator of Cyanilux's triangle mask, Ryan Zeng's G-channel end dissolve and the Battlerite width-over-life advice.
- A workable packing for a custom engine, consistent with the Ryan Zeng layout and the 5/6/5-bit note: R = streak shape (clamped along `u`), G = erosion noise (tiling, equalized, gets the 6-bit channel), B = distortion noise or a second streak layer. This exact layout is my suggestion, not a quoted convention.

### Gaps
- No source stated a typical texture resolution for slash/trail textures (256, 512 or otherwise). The only pixel sizes found were screenshot dimensions.
- No source showed a studio-authored slash texture with annotated channels for an anime or fighting game.

## 3. Shader: scrolling, erosion, colour ramp, distortion, blending, head/tail masking

### Takeaway
The recipe is: remap `u` so a clamped shape slides once along the arc (wipe-on), multiply by a scrolling noise for inner motion, erode with a threshold driven by age (step for hard cel edges, smoothstep for soft), fade the mesh borders with a UV-distance mask, then map the resulting grayscale through a colour ramp. Everything is driven by one or two 0-1 parameters animated by curves over the effect's lifetime.

### Cited Findings

**Wipe-on / single-pass scroll**
- [pro] Soulercoaster offset (Augie): take the UV, add -1 to the scroll channel so the texture starts outside the frame, add a per-particle dynamic parameter, sample with a clamped texture. The parameter's curve is two points, input 0 → output 0 and input 1 → output 2, so the offset travels from -1 to +1 over the particle's lifetime and the texture slides fully on and fully off. In formula: `uv.scroll = uv.scroll - 1 + p`, with `p = 2 * age01`. — [realtimevfx: VFX Basics - Mesh Soulercoasters](https://realtimevfx.com/t/vfx-basics-mesh-soulercoasters/1987)
- [tutorial] Cyanilux mask math with two parameters, `Slash` (position, default 0.5) and `Length` (visible length, slider 0-1, default 0.5): `x' = (uv.x - lerp(-Length, 1, Slash)) / Length`; sample `SlashTex` at `(x', uv.y)`; output the sample only where `0 <= x' <= 1`, otherwise 0. Animating `Slash` 0→1 moves the wedge from fully off one end to fully off the other; `Length` sets how much of the arc is covered. — [Cyanilux: Sword Slash Shader Breakdown](https://www.cyanilux.com/tutorials/sword-slash-shader-breakdown/)
- [pro] Scroll speed does not have to be constant: "you can key the scroll speed of the UV's over the particles lifetime" (Shannon Berke). — [realtimevfx: Shannon Berke LoL FX thread, page 6](https://realtimevfx.com/t/shannon-berke-lol-fx-knowledge-share/1133?page=6)

**Inner motion: scrolling noise layers**
- [pro] Shannon Berke's layered scroll: one texture scrolling at speed 1 and a second at 0.5 in the opposite direction (-0.5), multiplied together, with additive blending. — [realtimevfx: Shannon Berke LoL FX thread, page 6](https://realtimevfx.com/t/shannon-berke-lol-fx-knowledge-share/1133?page=6)
- [tutorial] Cyanilux noise pan: offset X = `-(Time * NoiseSpeed)` with `NoiseSpeed = 2`, tiling (1, 1); the mask, the noise sample and the edge fade are multiplied together, then multiplied by `Intensity = 20`. — [Cyanilux: Sword Slash Shader Breakdown](https://www.cyanilux.com/tutorials/sword-slash-shader-breakdown/)
- [pro] Diablo 3 formula (Julian Love via Nick Seavert): `Texture1.alpha * Texture2.alpha * 2`. Multiply by 2 only the product of panning noises, not the final shape mask. Use octave tiling scales 0.5, 1.0, 2.0 for layers; near-equal scales such as 0.8, 1.0, 1.2 cause visible phasing. Scroll speed of each layer is randomized per particle, clamped above zero up to 1. — [JangaFX: Diablo 3 VFX methods](https://jangafx.com/insights/diablo-3-vfx-experiments)
- [pro] A constantly scrolling gradient (rainbow in the example) over the trail material was used for the Battlerite-style trail (Limeslushie). — [realtimevfx: How to do this trail? Battlerite](https://realtimevfx.com/t/how-to-do-this-trail-battlerite/2943?page=2)

**Alpha erosion (dissolve)**
- [pro] Definition: determine "an erosion threshold in an alpha mask and animate this threshold ... so the shape dissolves itself into nothing". Hard version: `a = step(threshold, a)` (fully opaque or fully transparent, aliased edges). Soft version: `a = smoothstep(min, max, a)`, a feathered threshold. With feather > 0, thresholds of exactly 0 and 1 no longer fully reveal or fully erase, so remap the driving value to the range `[0 - feather, 1 + feather]`. — [vfxdoc: Alpha erosion](https://vfxdoc.readthedocs.io/en/latest/shaders/alpha-erosion/)
- [pro] Niels Dewitte's parametrization: `smoothstep(.2, .4, x)`, where values below .2 become 0 and above .4 become 1; expose a "Curve" (how far the cutout has progressed 0→1) and a "Soft" (feather width), combine with noise, feed to alpha. — [realtimevfx: Alpha/Opacity Mask/Clip/Erode workflow](https://realtimevfx.com/t/what-is-your-alpha-opacity-mask-clip-erode-animation-workflow-like/7705)
- [tutorial] Godot erosion node: `smoothstep(midpoint - half_range, midpoint + half_range, alpha)`, with the range clamped so exact 0 and 1 thresholds do not leave artifacts. From a search snippet. — [godotshaders: Alpha Erosion VisualShaderNode](https://godotshaders.com/shader/alpha-erosion-visualshadernode-4-4/)
- [pro] Other erosion variants from the same thread: subtract then multiply back up (harsh outline; simonschreibt); subtract → `ceil` → multiply by the original mask (hard edge; Travis); "Linear Burn" = subtract with `1 - x`, darkens as it fades; "Color Burn" = divide the result by the inverse of the subtracted amount, which keeps whites white and gives sharper edges (MikeH). Edge glow: route the inverse of the Linear Burn result into emissive so the rim glows where the alpha is about to disappear (MikeH). — [realtimevfx: Alpha/Opacity Mask/Clip/Erode workflow](https://realtimevfx.com/t/what-is-your-alpha-opacity-mask-clip-erode-animation-workflow-like/7705)
- [pro] Cheaper smoothstep: drop the clamp/Hermite part and keep only its linear remap. From a search snippet of a realtimevfx thread. — [realtimevfx: Getting smooth edges for alpha erosion](https://realtimevfx.com/t/getting-smooth-edged-for-alpha-erosion-for-masked/2897)
- [pro] Erosion that ramps along the trail: goal is erosion that "ramps up from 0 to 100% at the end of the trail"; Riot's engine does it by tracking the lifetime of each quad segment; in Unity the suggested custom shader uses "UV space * vertex.r & vertex.a" with an erosion map (Torbach). Shannon McSheehan's working answer: "Use the UV or color over trail, in stretch mode, to control the dissolve"; the shader "can use UV or vertex color". — [realtimevfx: Shannon Berke LoL FX thread, page 7](https://realtimevfx.com/t/shannon-berke-lol-fx-knowledge-share/1133?page=7)
- [pro] Erosion timing on a trail: "I have the erosion map keyed to start at .2 of the lifetime of the trail quads" (Shannon Berke, Riot). — [realtimevfx: Shannon Berke LoL FX thread, page 6](https://realtimevfx.com/t/shannon-berke-lol-fx-knowledge-share/1133?page=6)
- [pro] Drive erosion from per-particle age, not global time: use a relative-time/age-over-lifetime input; if the direction comes out reversed, invert the curve or use `1 - threshold`. From search snippets of Unity forum answers. — [Unity Discussions: Alpha-Clip Threshold over lifetime in VFX Graph](https://discussions.unity.com/t/how-to-set-alpha-clip-threshold-from-shader-graph-over-life-time-in-vfx/1512984)

**Head/tail and border masking**
- [tutorial] Cyanilux edge fade: `f = saturate(1 - abs(uv - 0.5) * float2(2.7, 2))`, then `fade = f.x * f.y`. The larger X factor makes the fade wider along the ring. Its purpose is to hide the mesh shape at its ends. — [Cyanilux: Sword Slash Shader Breakdown](https://www.cyanilux.com/tutorials/sword-slash-shader-breakdown/)
- [pro] When a panned texture reaches the edge of a ribbon's UVs it clips; three fixes (Travis): A) multiply alpha by a gradient so it fades before the edge; B) fade alpha to black at both the beginning and the end of the lifetime; C) do not pan, use a seamless tile or a static shape. Option A worked for the asker, but the gradient made the trail start "way behind the projectile", fixed by offsetting the texture in Photoshop. — [realtimevfx: Shannon Berke LoL FX thread, page 7](https://realtimevfx.com/t/shannon-berke-lol-fx-knowledge-share/1133?page=7)
- [pro] Protecting the head: Ryan Zeng multiplies both the distortion strength and the dissolve mask by a gradient along U so the start of the trail keeps its shape and full opacity while distortion and dissolve increase toward the end; an extra edge mask fades the trail where strong flow pushes it past its area. — [realtimevfx: RzFX Flow trail breakdown](https://realtimevfx.com/t/rzfx-flow-trail-breakdown/6483)
- [pro] Riot style guide (via summary): the leading edge of a moving element should have the highest brightness and the sharpest contrast boundary, which pulls the eye forward. — [VFX Apprentice: 10 LoL VFX design tips](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips) [secondary]

**UV distortion**
- [pro] Distortion = a noise channel remapped from 0..1 to -1..+1 and added to the UVs, scaled down to a small range such as -0.1..+0.1 so the wobble stays subtle (Torbach). Two channels can drive X and Y separately. — [realtimevfx: Most common bread & butter shaders](https://realtimevfx.com/t/most-common-bread-butter-shaders-and-materials-techniques/19127)
- [pro] Ryan Zeng remaps the noise with a bias/scale node from 0-1 to -1-1 so the distorted trail stays centred. — [realtimevfx: RzFX Flow trail breakdown](https://realtimevfx.com/t/rzfx-flow-trail-breakdown/6483)
- [pro] Refraction-only slash (Nier Automata-style): Erik Forsström removed the colour so the trail is grayscale and relies on refraction, and animated a white flash on the trail at the moment of impact. — [realtimevfx: Erik Forsström Sketch #14](https://realtimevfx.com/t/erik-forsstrom-sketch-14-wip/5167)

**Colour: gradient mapping**
- [pro] Gradient mapping: a grayscale value indexes a colour gradient/LUT so one element can show several colours; "Color LUTs are super powerful, and especially important for stylized vfx" (Travis). — [realtimevfx: Most common bread & butter shaders](https://realtimevfx.com/t/most-common-bread-butter-shaders-and-materials-techniques/19127)
- [tutorial] Cyanilux: the (mask × noise × fade × 20) value is the `t` of a lerp between two colour properties; he recommends a 3-way gradient or gradient texture for more control, and uses HDR + bloom + ACES tonemapping for the glow. A `Step` property of (0, 0.1) feeds a smoothstep on the same intensity value (where its output connects is shown only in an image, most likely the alpha). — [Cyanilux: Sword Slash Shader Breakdown](https://www.cyanilux.com/tutorials/sword-slash-shader-breakdown/)
- [pro] Geri's slash gets its colour from "a simple Color Ramp + Glow", with bloom post-processing on. — [realtimevfx: Venom Slash breakdown](https://realtimevfx.com/t/venom-slash-breakdown-of-the-effect-included/18903)
- [pro] Two-colour blend from start to end of the trail; multiplying colour by opacity turns partially transparent pixels dark, which gives an optional dark edge (Ryan Zeng). — [realtimevfx: RzFX Flow trail breakdown](https://realtimevfx.com/t/rzfx-flow-trail-breakdown/6483)

**Blending**
- [pro] Additive: Shannon Berke's layered trail explicitly uses "the additive blend mode". — [realtimevfx: Shannon Berke LoL FX thread, page 6](https://realtimevfx.com/t/shannon-berke-lol-fx-knowledge-share/1133?page=6)
- [pro] Alpha blend: the Unity trail shader discussed for the same technique appears to use `SrcAlpha OneMinusSrcAlpha` (Torbach reading the shared graph). — [realtimevfx: Shannon McSheehan LoL FX thread, post 168](https://realtimevfx.com/t/shannon-berke-lol-fx-knowledge-share/1133/168)
- [secondary] Riot style guide summary: avoid absolute extremes of value (0% black, 100% white); mid values form the body and the highest value spike is reserved for the brief primary flash. — [VFX Apprentice: 10 LoL VFX design tips](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips)

### Inferences
- The sources disagree on whether a single mesh can erode non-uniformly. Shannon Berke says one mesh makes "the whole texture" erode uniformly and uses per-quad lifetime on ribbons. Torbach and Shannon McSheehan show the workaround: feed the shader a value that varies along the trail (UV in stretch mode, or vertex colour over the trail) and use it to bias the erosion threshold. Cyanilux and Qriva avoid the issue by sliding a tapered mask. Both positions are compatible: a single mesh erodes uniformly only if the threshold is one global number.
- Combined per-pixel recipe for a static arc mesh, assembled from the cited pieces (my synthesis; no single source gives it in this form):

```
// inputs: uv (u along arc 0..1 in swing direction, v across width), t = effect age 0..1
// curves: reveal(t) 0->1 fast; erode(t) 0 until ~0.2 then ->1; len = visible arc fraction
head  = lerp(-len, 1.0, reveal(t))            // Cyanilux: lerp(-Length, 1, Slash)
x     = (uv.u - head) / len                   // 0 at tail of visible window, 1 at head
shape = (x >= 0 && x <= 1) ? tex(R, vec2(x, uv.v)) : 0   // clamped wedge/streaks
d     = (tex(B, uv*tile + scrollB*time) * 2 - 1) * 0.1 * (1 - x)   // distortion, zero at head
n     = tex(G, vec2(uv.u*tileU - scroll*time, uv.v) + d)           // scrolling noise
border = saturate(1 - abs(uv - 0.5) * vec2(2.7, 2.0)); border = border.x * border.y
body  = shape * border
// erosion threshold grows with global age and toward the tail (x small)
thr   = erode(t) * (1 + feather) + (1 - x) * tailBias - feather
alpha = smoothstep(thr, thr + feather, body * n)       // feather ~0 for cel look
color = ramp(saturate(body * n * intensity))           // grayscale -> palette LUT
```

- For a hard-edged anime look the same graph is used with `feather` near zero (step), and the ramp replaced by 2-3 flat bands. This follows from the step/smoothstep distinction in vfxdoc plus the gradient-map practice; no source states it for slashes specifically.
- Additive vs alpha blend: the sources show both in use and none gives a rule for slashes. Additive (Shannon Berke) suits glowing, light-like trails and cannot darken the background. A cel-style opaque white or coloured blade with a dark edge (Ryan Zeng's colour × opacity trick) needs alpha blending. This reading is mine.

### Gaps
- No source gave a scroll speed in physical units for a slash (only relative values: 1 and -0.5 for layer speeds, `NoiseSpeed = 2` UV units per second in Cyanilux's graph).
- The exact node graphs for Shannon Berke's and Erik Forsström's trails exist only as images, which could not be read.
- Nothing found on how commercial anime-style games (Genshin, Honkai, Arc System Works titles) author their slash shaders.

## 4. Animation and timing

### Takeaway
The few concrete numbers found put a whole slash trail at roughly 0.2-0.6 s (12-36 frames at 60 fps), with erosion starting at about 20% of each trail section's life rather than fading uniformly. The appear phase is a fast wipe along the arc; disappearing is done by erosion and width change from the tail, and artists adjust perceived speed by shortening the trail rather than speeding it up.

### Cited Findings
- [pro] Erik Forsström's slash sketch: total time until the slash is completely gone was reduced "from 0.6s to 0.4s"; earlier he "almost doubled the speed of the slash"; finally "I made the slash trail shorter instead of faster, because I think that was the problem with the speed". At 60 fps, 0.6 s = 36 frames and 0.4 s = 24 frames. — [realtimevfx: Erik Forsström Sketch #14](https://realtimevfx.com/t/erik-forsstrom-sketch-14-wip/5167)
- [pro] Shannon Berke (Riot): erosion keyed "to start at .2 of the lifetime of the trail quads"; the trail was emitted using particle lifetime rather than distance ("Time or distance is up to the look you're going for. I used particle lifetime"); "Color over life is good to have a fade out over the emitter or particle lifetime". — [realtimevfx: Shannon Berke LoL FX thread, page 6](https://realtimevfx.com/t/shannon-berke-lol-fx-knowledge-share/1133?page=6) and [page 7](https://realtimevfx.com/t/shannon-berke-lol-fx-knowledge-share/1133?page=7)
- [pro] On a ribbon with alpha dissolve over life, "the trail will only dissolve at the end of its life", i.e. dissolution proceeds from the oldest section (Kris_Wilkins). — [realtimevfx: Shannon Berke LoL FX thread, page 7](https://realtimevfx.com/t/shannon-berke-lol-fx-knowledge-share/1133?page=7)
- [pro] Width animates over life: "scaled the trails width over its lifetime", plus the trail moves outward and grows slightly while rotating (Limeslushie). — [realtimevfx: How to do this trail? Battlerite](https://realtimevfx.com/t/how-to-do-this-trail-battlerite/2943?page=2)
- [pro] The slash is revealed "in sync with the sword swing" (AW_VFX); scroll and erosion are driven by curves over the particle lifetime (Qriva); a spinning slash mesh's rotation is controlled by plotting points on a rotation-over-life graph (veer). — [realtimevfx: General question about sword slash vfx](https://realtimevfx.com/t/general-question-about-sword-slash-vfx/26396); [Unity Discussions: anime style attacks](https://discussions.unity.com/t/how-do-i-make-vfx-for-attacks-in-anime-style/879490); [realtimevfx: Sword Slash help](https://realtimevfx.com/t/sword-slash-help/9407)
- [tutorial] Cyanilux animates the `_Slash` and `_Length` material properties (and scale) with keyframes in the animation window alongside the sword animation, and disables the renderer when the effect is not playing. — [Cyanilux: Sword Slash Shader Breakdown](https://www.cyanilux.com/tutorials/sword-slash-shader-breakdown/)
- [secondary] Riot style guide timing, per VFX Apprentice's summary: a non-linear three-stage curve of brief anticipation, an impact that peaks "within the first quarter of the effect's lifespan", then a dissipation that clears; linear timing is discouraged; "If Your FX Feel Long, They're Waaaaay Too Long". The quoted fraction comes from the summary, not from the guide itself, which I could not open. — [VFX Apprentice: 10 LoL VFX design tips](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips)
- [pro] Feedback pattern on slash work: "most of this comes down to timing and secondary effects showing impact"; "make the trails a tad larger for a little more impact" (Cundall77, LilSpence). — [realtimevfx: Sword Slash help](https://realtimevfx.com/t/sword-slash-help/9407)
- [snippet] Forum critique of a trail that "dies off a little suddenly", with the suggestion to add a secondary faint trail as aftermath. Search snippet; the specific thread was not opened. — [realtimevfx: Sword slash combo, advice welcome](https://realtimevfx.com/t/sword-slash-combo-advice-are-really-welcome/9379)
- [secondary] Stepped animation in an anime-style fighting game: Guilty Gear Xrd runs at 60 fps but uses limited animation where each frame is a hand-posed key with no interpolation; its hit effects are 2D image sequences that switch every frame. — [videohighlight summary of the GDC talk](https://videohighlight.com/v/yhGjCzxJV3E)
- [hobby] A three.js slash trail used a random per-particle lifetime of 0.2 to 0.3 s with shrink and fade over life (search snippet). — [dev.to: three.js sword-slash trail](https://dev.to/sam_novak_574b07811e18495/a-threejs-sword-slash-trail-on-a-running-character-mount-it-on-the-scene-or-on-the-hero-1305)

### Inferences
- A timeline consistent with the numbers above, for a 60 fps game (my construction, to be tuned by eye): total life 24 frames (0.4 s, Forsström's final value). Reveal runs 0→1 over the swing's active frames (first ~25% of life, matching the "first quarter" peak in the Riot summary). Erosion threshold stays at 0 until 20% of life (Berke) and then rises to 1 by the end, biased so the tail goes first. Width narrows toward the end.
- "Thinning, not uniform fade" is supported indirectly: erosion from the oldest section (Kris_Wilkins/Berke), width scaled over life (Limeslushie), and the tapered mask. None of the sources recommends a plain alpha fade as the main exit; colour-over-life fades are mentioned only as a complement.
- Stepped updates for the trail itself: because Xrd holds character poses and switches 2D hit frames discretely, quantizing the trail's `reveal` and `erode` parameters to the same cadence as the character's pose changes would keep the effect in step with the animation. No source confirms that any game does this for sword trails.

### Gaps
- No source gave frame counts for swing-trail lifetime in a shipped fighting or anime game, nor hold counts (twos/threes) for effects.
- No source discussed anticipation or overshoot specifically for slash trails. The Riot guide's anticipation/impact/dissipation model is the nearest, and I only have it second-hand.
- No example curve graphs could be extracted; the curves in forum posts are images.

## 5. Stylization: how anime-style ("cel") trails differ

### Takeaway
Public documentation of anime-style game VFX is thin. What is supported: anime-style fighting games author effects frame by frame (2D image sequences, per-frame meshes), keep them simple, unlit and without outlines for readability, and replace motion blur with authored smears and exaggeration. The hard-edge, flat-tone look itself is achieved with the same shader blocks as soft effects (step instead of smoothstep, a banded colour ramp).

### Cited Findings
- [secondary] Guilty Gear Xrd chose limited animation because full animation "made the visuals appear more 3D rather than maintaining a 2D feel"; each frame is a keyframe like stop motion; hit effects are 2D images switching every frame; jump smoke is one modelled mesh per frame. — [videohighlight summary of the GDC talk](https://videohighlight.com/v/yhGjCzxJV3E)
- [snippet] Xrd effects (via Polycount relay of 4Gamer): billboard smoke did not look convincing, so each smoke frame is its own mesh; most effects are simple, have no outlines and do not cast light on surrounding objects, to keep the fight readable. — [Polycount: Convincing 3d that looks like 2d, p5](https://polycount.com/discussion/121144/convincing-3d-that-looks-like-2d-wow/p5)
- [pro] Hard edge vs soft edge is a one-function difference: `step(threshold, a)` gives fully opaque or fully transparent pixels; `smoothstep(min, max, a)` gives a feathered edge. Opaque alpha-clipped shapes also avoid overdraw. — [vfxdoc: Alpha erosion](https://vfxdoc.readthedocs.io/en/latest/shaders/alpha-erosion/)
- [pro] "good vfx is usually not about particles, but shaders and textures"; for anime attacks use hand-painted textures or flipbooks as masks, scrolled UVs and erosion (Qriva). — [Unity Discussions: anime style attacks](https://discussions.unity.com/t/how-do-i-make-vfx-for-attacks-in-anime-style/879490)
- [pro] Smears replace motion blur and allow deliberate distortion, with anime cited as the influence (David Gibson, Overwatch). — [Kotaku: This Is Not An Overwatch Glitch](https://kotaku.com/this-is-not-an-overwatch-glitch-1777612546)
- [secondary] Riot style guide summary: primary elements get high value, clear silhouettes, high opacity and a sharp focal point; secondary elements get lower values, reduced opacity and simpler blurred shapes; hand-drawn shapes are preferred; an effect's size and intensity should match the importance of the attack. — [VFX Apprentice: 10 LoL VFX design tips](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips)
- [pro] Size: "make the trails a tad larger for a little more impact" (LilSpence); a user pulled the trail's end socket beyond the sword tip to enlarge the trail. — [realtimevfx: Sword Slash help](https://realtimevfx.com/t/sword-slash-help/9407)
- [pro] Afterimages ("onion skinning" / "after image"), approaches listed by VFX artists: 2D, tinted screen-grab sprites of the character placed along the path (NateLane); 3D, baked pose meshes ("mesh flipbooks") spawned as mesh particles (Travis), triggered by animation notifies for exact timing, with "a nice additive fresnel material" that fades out over time (tharlevfx); Unity `BakeMesh` + `Graphics.DrawMesh` (Squirrely_Jones); render target capturing only the character, which is not 3D (hyawatha15); copies can also shrink while fading (kupokitty). Street Fighter-style afterimages "basically stop and stand still over time" (powerstroke3000). — [realtimevfx: Mega Man / Street Fighter afterimage effect](https://realtimevfx.com/t/how-do-you-call-that-mega-man-street-fighter-afterimage-effect-and-how-would-you-do-it/3659)
- [snippet] Afterimage tuning tips from Unreal community sources: emit by time or by distance, cap the number of copies per trail, and skip emission when the position delta is too low. Search snippets, partly vendor listings. — [Unreal forums: Mustafa Kum Modular AfterImage FX](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/mustafa-kum-modular-afterimage-fx/2849613)

### Inferences
- Cel version of the trail shader: threshold the same `shape × noise` value twice (for example a core band and an edge band) and assign each band one flat colour; this is gradient mapping with a stepped ramp. It gives the "white core, coloured edge, hard outline" look without new textures. Derived from the step/gradient-map findings; not quoted from an anime-game source.
- In a 2D sprite game the afterimage is cheap: the "tinted sprite copies along the motion path" method (NateLane) is literally redrawing earlier frames of the character with a flat tint and fading or shrinking them. Leaving the copies fixed in world space matches the Street Fighter observation.
- The cited sources support the view that, in anime-style fighting games, readability rules (no outlines on effects, no lighting contribution, simple shapes) matter more than shader complexity.

### Gaps
- No source found on speed lines, halftone/coloured silhouette afterimages, or the 2-3 flat tone convention as used in specific anime games (Naruto Storm, DBFZ, Genshin, Honkai). These are widely observed but I found no artist or studio statement to cite.
- No numbers for how large a trail should be relative to the character.
- The Hi-Fi Rush GDC 2024 talk ("3D Toon Rendering in Hi-Fi RUSH") exists but its content on effects could not be accessed; whether it covers stepped VFX is unknown.
